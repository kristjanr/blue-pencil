I sat back in my command chair, put my hands behind my head, and stretched. I was in a mani in real, and I had to admit it felt a lot more real than we'd ever been able to manage in VR. Whether that was a shortcoming in our VR programming or simply the progress in neurological verisimilitude since Hueys were created, I couldn't say.

And I honestly didn't care. Charles was in a display window checking something off-screen. He reached off-camera, then said,

Uh-huh. About 15,000 new applications for immigration-to-soul system from around the USF. And it's not slowing down.

To Earth? Charles grimaced. Mostly.

But we've got a few hundred applying for lunar settlement and 60-odd for Mars. I can understand Luna. I mean, the moon is now at least marginally habitable, even if the air is still a bit thin.

I nodded. I'd had something similar with Valhalla early on as well, people trying to get in on the ground floor before it became a thing. But Charles was still talking.

But Mars is nowhere near ready. I mean, we've got the temps up and we're dropping chunks of ice on it, but it's nowhere near livable yet. You'd be under a dome for at least a few more decades.

Hmm, I responded distractedly. And Venus? Charles laughed.

That's still a hellhole. We've siphoned off about half the atmosphere and the temps are dropping thanks to the solar shade, but there's still a long way to go. No takers there, I sat forward.

Well, look, if people want to settle the moon, great, have at it, but I think we're going to have to say no to Mars. It would add a lot of oversight requirements with no real payoff at this point, and hell, people have the whole damned earth to choose from. Uh-huh.

What about letting people spread out, Will? I considered that for a moment. If enough people are able to get together to form a viable settlement group, fine.

Otherwise, sane comment applies. We don't want to have to split our support systems up in too many directions at once, and construction will never keep up. Charles got a funny look on his face at my last comment, but before I could ask about it, he nodded and settled back in his chair with an air of finality.

That's it for today. Want to go for a hike? I was slightly taken aback by this suggestion, but recovered quickly.

Well, why not? Anywhere in particular? I'm thinking Vegas.

I... Vegas? I looked askance at Charles in the video window, but his expression was deadpan.

That was a dead giveaway. He had something up his sleeve. Okay, Charles, I'll play straight man.

Meet you in half an hour? Good. Out.

There weren't a lot of spare mannies on Earth, so I would have to physically fly myself down.

I could have, I suppose, flown down using my mannies built-in surge drive, but for some reason I'd picked up Bill's attitude about secrecy, so I didn't want to advertise the capability unless necessary. And all the shuttles were in high demand with the colonization effort, so that meant bundling my mannie in the hold of a cargo drone like so much inventory. Not the most dignified mode of travel.

I somehow arrived in Las Vegas before Charles, so I sat down in the ruins of a parking lot. Vegas hadn't actually been ground under by- high-glacier, instead merely enduring several decades of accumulating snowfall, but that had been

but that had been enough to destroy a lot of infrastructure. Frost heaves and then the weight of successive layers of snow had cracked and shifted any flat stretches of concrete, and many buildings hadn't had the proper shape to shed snow. A lot of them had caved in.

But the city was still recognizable. Both the Luxor and the Sphere had come out mostly unscathed, and many of the signs on the strip were mainly vertical. I looked up as a shuttle came in for a landing.

Charles raided his own personal craft by the simple expedient of having manufactured it himself. I added a to-do item to copy his strategy and maybe build an extra manny to go with it.

Charles exited his shuttle, waved and jogged over. Hey, Will, busy times, eh? No kidding.

This is as good? I stopped and gave him the stink eye. Okay, no.

You have an agenda. Let's skip to the punchline.

Charles grinned at me. You know, this is a lot more fun with the humans. They're easier to string along.

Uh-huh, give. Notice anything different? Charles waved expansively to emphasize the point.

Well, the town has become a lot more run down and traffic's certainly lighter, I grinned and Charles chuckled, but original Bob was only here for a short while and he spent most of his time at the con functions. How about a hint? Okay, Vegas used to be desert.

I jerked and looked around with fresh eyes. I hadn't noticed at first, but there were a lot of trees. Very probably far more than Vegas had ever enjoyed in the before times.

Okay, you seeded the place with trees. I know the climate is better because you've been making a point of shrinking desert areas, so is there a moral to the story? Uh-huh.

Most of these trees are less than three months old. The trees in question were up to 50 feet in height, so that seemed unlikely, but Charles wasn't just going to tell a whopper for fun. Force grown?

I asked. Nope. Printed.

Excuse the hell out of me? I exclaimed. Now Charles' grin was full wattage.

We can print trees and most types of plants on the spot. Observe. Charles gestured to my right where some kind of weird craft had just appeared and was settling over a grassy boulevard area.

The craft, it wasn't a shuttle or a drone, more like a giant floating printer, slowed to a hover about 20 feet above the stretch of ground. A ring on the underside started glowing with what I recognized as a tensor field generator. A cylinder of force formed, looking very much like a Star Trek transporter, and inside it, a tree appeared and solidified.

The whole operation couldn't have taken more than five seconds before the field blinked off and the craft flew away That was obviously a prearranged demo, Charles said, delight in his voice We still have to seed plants the old-fashioned way as well because the printed versions are infertile

Infertile? I exclaimed Charles waved it off, plainly not concerned

It's a known issue and it'll probably be fixed in the next patch release But the printed trees still act like trees They give shade

They anchor the earth and retain water with their root systems They inhale carbon dioxide and exhale oxygen Over time, the natural ones will replace them as they replicate the old-fashioned way

Wow! I exclaimed, totally thunderstruck I hadn't realized the technology had gotten to that point

Then I frowned Wait a minute, where does the raw material come from? We're still not able to create matter out of energy, are we?

Wormholes, Charles said with a grin The printer connects to a central reservoir using wormholes and draws elements as required I shook my head in wonder

Again, wow Intellectually, I understand that I can't keep up with everything, but still That seems like something I'd have heard about.

Things are changing on an almost weekly basis now, Will. You take a nap at the wrong time and you'll miss something. And we can print structures as well.

We're going to scan the buildings your colonists are putting up and just start duplicating them. I've got some colonists working on CAD designs for different types of buildings and infrastructure, like bridges and such. I know you're a little worried about all the late applicants, but don't be.

We'll have a place for them. I grinned and looked around. Welcome to Earth version 2.
