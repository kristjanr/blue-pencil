It had been four months, and we had all agreed to meet back at the EntryHub portal to compare notes, and although no one had come out and said it, if someone turned up missing, this would be when we would find out. I got a surprise when Day and I got to the last couple of portals. I was suddenly on BobNet.

I cast around for a moment, scanning the area, and Day broke in. Hey, Icky, I'm connected to BobNet. Did Bill succeed with the membership application?

Doesn't look like it. I've detected a couple of satellites with standard BobNet. Bobiverse Operating Systems, Maser-based comms from the looks of it.

So we just rolled our own? Didn't we decide that would be too much trouble? Well, you and I decided that, the rest of the Bobiverse may have had other ideas, but seriously, this feels like something Bill would do if we got rejected.

Why the bleep would they reject us? How the hell would I know? Maybe 42 is the wrong answer.

Yeah, okay, or maybe Bill just got impatient. In any case, the blog says he's left a supply of satellites in the Malacca system along with deployment instructions. We should grab a bunch before we go out again.

It took another day or so to get to Malacca where we found the expected satellites all bundled up in deployable pairs with a convenient launching shell. Gotta love manufacturing standards.

We took the time to read Bill's blog and got caught up on the whole application fiasco.

Digitals, were we? Not good enough for civilized society?

I couldn't decide if I was more amused or offended.

I'm sure Bill is working on it, Icky, Dae said when I muttered a complaint. At that moment, Calvin and Goku popped through the wormhole. Let the bells ring!

We have arrived, Goku announced. Just as soon as I finish yawning, I sent back. We're early.

It's just the four of us so far. Over the next several hours, Bobs popped through the wormhole portal until we had all 20 Bobs accounted for.

I see no one has gotten eaten by space whales, Elmer observed. Has anyone seen any space whales? One of the other Bobs asked.

A chorus of no's came back, along with a comment from one Bob that they hadn't seen space anythings, and unless you love scenery, this adventure was a total bust. Well, not total, Calvin said. We found an alien derelict and a portal to a globular cluster, NGC-6934, and almost got eaten by a black hole.

Really? Suddenly everyone in the room was all ears. Well, almost is relative, Goku admitted.

100 AU away. Goku proceeded to give an abbreviated account of our adventures. I gotcha beat, announced Dylan.

He was about 20th generation, somewhere around Herschel and Neil's generation, and you could feel just by his tone that he wasn't quite Bob anymore. Okay, Dylan, lay it on us.

The PGF apparently does have portals to weird systems. We found one circling a neutron star. Way the hell out, of course.

Couple hundred AU. Where was it? I asked.

The portal, I mean.

Second to last hub around Ring 3. 364 is the designation.

Interesting that Calvin's globular cluster portal and your neutron star portal were way out in the boonies.

Your point? It's like the PGF went about closing down weird system portals and missed a couple. Why would they do that?

Hell, I don't know, but it's as good an explanation as anything else we've come up with. Especially, Elmer observed, since we haven't come up with anything else. Well, there is that.

Actually, Elmer added, the spinward edge of the network is really ragged. I wonder if they deliberately went about removing portals at that end. Again?

Why? Again? Don't know, Elmer gave us a jaundiced look.

Let me know when you figure it out.
