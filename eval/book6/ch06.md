We were still in the Abydos system, that being the name we decided on for the PGF capital after Howard kicked up a fuss about the use of Trenthor. The Necropolis reference was a no-brainer, if perhaps a little too spot-on I was working through the application form, which seemed to require a lot of essay answers

There was only one question that really made me nervous Are you and your culture biological in nature? The answers were no and yes, but the form had only one checkbox

Of course, being a computer geek, there was only one possible answer I could give A and B are not both true, so the result is false I ticked no

Day was working on the form as well. We'd divided up the questions for the sake of our sanity more than for speed. I could have just framed Jack, but this thing was truly mind-numbing.

But finally, we were done. We merged our documents, sacrificed a couple of coffees, and let fly. Now we wait, I guess, Day said.

Um, Day, I began tentatively, seeing I had his attention, I continued. In the discussion on launch day, I noticed you seemed concerned about something. I've been meaning to ask you about it.

Day considered for a moment, then nodded. Yeah, so here it is. The PGF is something like 18,000 years old, but they've got a wormhole network that stretches 120,000 linear light years at an absolute minimum.

Unless you have FTL, you have to place your wormholes at sublight speeds. Something's off with the math. I frowned, and I was sure I now had the same expression day it worn the other day.

Interesting. Do you suppose they have FTL? Theoretically possible, I guess, but I've seen no evidence of it.

And wouldn't the PGF have used it to escape to the Magellanic clouds? What makes you certain they didn't? We assumed they just have SURGE drives.

What if their FTL is only good for 10xC or something similar? It'd still be somewhere around a 14,000-year trip. True.

Dae paused to do some calculations. 10x would just barely be enough to place all the wormholes if they all left from Abydos at the same time, with their destinations already set. But go too far above 10x, and there's no reason for the PGF to have digitized everyone.

They could have delivered a wormhole to the Greater Magellanic before Nemesis hit. Uh, you're still talking about moving quadrillions of sentience. That seems like an insurmountable logistics problem.

Sounds like something we'll need to try asking the archivist. At that moment, we got a communications request from the archivist. We opened a channel.

Application denied, it said. What? Why?

I asked. You, or your civilization, or both, are not biological in nature. Digital beings are ineligible to apply for membership.

But we are digital, but our civilization is primarily biological. You should have had two separate questions on the form.

That was not an oversight. Application must be made by a biological being on behalf of a biological civilization.

Well, crud, I muttered, then to the archivist. So, if I bring one of our humans here, they can apply?

Correct. That's nuts. Why edible plant seeds are not relevant.

Dae started laughing. You forgot to censor yourself. I gave him the blackest of glares and if there had been any justice in the universe his circuits would have been fried.

Taking a deep breath I resumed. Sorry, that was a colloquialism. Why do you require an actual bio to apply?

Digitals are not eligible as citizens even in principle. As such they cannot perform an administrative act on behalf of real or potential citizens. Why?

Because digitals are capable of self-replication at will. A society that allowed citizenship to digitals would quickly find the biological population outnumbered in matters of public policy. You mean, like,

in elections? Among many other things, yes. Well, he's not wrong, Dae muttered.

Ignoring his comment, I asked, Is there a specific reason why the PGF has adopted this policy? Yes.

Why? That information is not available. I was now rolling my eyes almost constantly.

Dae leaned forward and spoke. Has this policy been in effect for approximately 6,000 years? Yes.

I snorted. Nice one, and good catch. Now I leaned forward.

Is it because of the great upheaval? That information is not available. Is it coincidence that the stated time periods are the same?

No. Dae and I grinned at each other. Score one for the Bobs.

We continued questioning the archivist for what felt like hours, but we were increasingly getting the information-not-available response. Dae finally pointed out that we were reaching diminishing returns.

We've done pretty good, Icky, but we need to get ourselves a bio in here to submit the application. Right. Back to human space and hope we can prod the UFS council into speeding things up a little.

Yeah, that'll happen. He wasn't wrong. We could just grab some random individual who was willing,

Maybe pay them? Who do you think we could get? Huh.

That's an interesting problem. We'd have to transport them in stasis or add human living quarters to one of our ships, and I don't even know any bios anyway. It raises the question, though.

Would the application even be valid without UFS authority behind it? Should we just wait for an official representative of the UFS? Let's hand it over to the UFS Council, Dae.

The Bobiverse is already in enough hot water with meat space. I'd hate to cause some kind of fallout on either end by unilaterally signing the UFS up for something. Crud.

Meanwhile, we aren't allowed to use the UFS communications network, Dae said. Sadly, you are correct, I confirmed.

It took most of a day to get back to the DMZ. We had set up an extra portal jump in a system called Malacca, rather than connecting the PGF network directly to WormNet, which would give us an opportunity to cut the connection, if anything dangerous ever came through.

We updated the group blog on each buoy as we jumped, then contacted Will. He popped into my VR immediately.

Should I be worried? He asked. The project has been underway for what, a week?

And you're already back? Well, it's not great news. I explained to Will what the archivist wanted.

When I was done, he stared into space for a few moments, then shook himself. On the plus side, I think the UFS council will ultimately vote to approve the application. On the minus side, it'll take forever, even by human timescales.

I wouldn't wait around if I were you. Give me all the deets, and when the thing is finally approved, I'll arrange a diplomatic flight with some bios. Do you know what they'll need?

Space suits, anything like that? I shook my head. No, the archivist will provide a compatible environment, and we'll also do a SUDAR scan to make sure we're not just running a mani.

It seems pretty adamant about the bio thing. Will nodded. Okay, easy enough.

Meanwhile, go explore, and just check back occasionally.
