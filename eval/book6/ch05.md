Bridget and I came in for a smooth landing at the edge of the floater right by the village. A couple of dragons hopped sideways to make room before launching themselves on whatever personal journeys they had. I took a deep breath, enjoying the slightly farm-like aroma of a dragon village on a floater.

It was good to be back, even if this was business. For Bridget, anyway. We'd modified the appearance of our drannies and picked a floater that we'd never visited.

It would absolutely not be good to be recognized, not that Alexander and his armies were a force anymore. He had, as we'd hoped, packed up and retired once the dragon exodus to Lemuria had successfully concluded. According to surveillance, he had set up his own kingdom, or duchy, or whatever, on his floater and was reverting to a life of quiet scholarship.

My opinion of him went up slightly with this news. In fact, things were going so well overall that Bridget and her fellow biologists were having fits trying to figure out why there hadn't been dragons and floaters on Lemuria already.

We spent some time renting a place, getting some to-ev and lunch, and just chatting with random individuals at the eatery. The mood was upbeat and optimistic. One person summed it up perfectly.

The fish is plentiful here. The floaters seem content. There's lots of room, and I haven't smelled a single fumarole fart since we got here.

What's not to like? Don't much care for the taste of the local fish, an elder interjected from two perches over. I'll be happy when the trout population breeds up.

Assuming they do, a third said. Wasn't much left by the time the floaters got here. That triggered a debate about fish, stocks, and distribution that made me order more to have just for the numbing effect.

Bridget, of course, was all over it, completely glued to the conversation. She messaged me at one point. It's really surprising how knowledgeable the dragons are about fish breeding and populations and such.

It's like they developed expertise in this one specialized aspect of biology. I gave that some thought, then replied, The dragons seem like that in general, though. In some areas they're quite advanced for what I'd characterize as a Stone Age society, and other places they're way behind.

Bridget's response came back right away. Howard, I'd put them at about ancient Babylon level in general. They just don't do a lot of the ziggurat level construction projects because they have the float, er, leviathans.

I smiled at her and she gave me a low growl. Bridget was still resisting the nickname, but it was a losing battle, just based on syllable count.

We finished our lunch, then decided to go for a flight. Launching ourselves from our perch, we headed straight out from the floater. It was a minor breach of behavior, about equivalent to jaywalking.

We climbed as quickly as we could get away with and leveled out at a comfortable maximum for a bio-dragon, then headed east. It was a beautiful day, sun shining on the land, the occasional cloud fighting to avoid evaporation, and only one or two other floaters in sight. I pulled up beside Bridget and said, They seem to have dispersed pretty thoroughly.

Hmm. It's a small concern. Leviathans don't reproduce quickly, but dragons do.

It's either going to get very crowded in a few decades, or the dragons will have to learn to live on the ground. I looked down at the land below us. Not a lot of big trees.

That could be a problem. Bridget followed my gaze. You're right.

The Talf species all live commensally on the Leviathans. I wonder if we need to do something about that. Prime directive, love.

My ass, Howard. You bobs pay about as much attention to that as Kirk did. Agape, Speechless, Then Grinned, Trek Lore

Yet one more reason why I loved her But still A tree breeding program to replace floaters

Bob's favorite complaint echoed in the back of my head Bob's really couldn't leave things alone Even the honorary ones

Bridget was silent for several minutes And I was happy to enjoy the flight The closest I'd ever felt to this experience when I was human

Was the one time I'd accepted an invitation to go soaring with Alan Huffington The memory of my friend and former employee caused an unexpected pang of regret.

I hadn't thought of Carl, Karen, and Alan in, literally, centuries. Once again, I was reminded that there were downsides to immortality. Alan had been a dedicated sailplane pilot, and after that one experience, I understood why.

The quiet, the feeling of being able to touch the sky was like nothing else I'd ever experienced. I remembered wondering if this was what it felt like to be an eagle. Flying a dranny had answered that question for me, and the answer was no.

Gliding, as enjoyable as it had been, was a pale imitation of this. On a sailplane, you were still a passenger. As a dragon, or an eagle, I supposed, you engaged directly with the wind, the updrafts, and the air currents.

I grinned a little as I thought of my latest business venture. Bridget rolled her eyes whenever I started talking about the adventure park, but I knew now it couldn't help but be another huge success. Bridget interrupted my musing.

There will be wars, Howard.

What? The non sequitur would be confusing, even if I hadn't been yanked out of my own thoughts. The dragons will breed faster than the floaties, er, floaters.

There won't be enough living space for all. They will fight over the floaters. This is inevitable and disastrous.

The dragons could still be wiped out, but by their own hand. I looked over at Bridget, gliding ten feet off my starboard wing. The translator automatically interpreted her expression for me.

She was really worried.

What can we do, though, Bridge? We can't bring back Alexander, and I'm not sure that would be the right move anyway. Even ignoring the prime directive, there are limits to our power.

Bridget frowned more at the problem than at me. Maybe. The tree-breeding program would fix things eventually, but trees don't grow any faster here than they did on Earth, or Vulcan.

It will probably be too little, too late. I suppressed a sigh. There went my high.

But if Bridget was concerned about it, I was going to be concerned as well.
