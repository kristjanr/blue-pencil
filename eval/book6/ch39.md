Theresa and I were back on New PAV in our Quinneys and had an appointment with the Planetary Governor, Pazila Bahir, and their Commander-in-Chief, Admiral Gauz Palaz. The PAV kept a rigid separation between the Executive and Military branches to the point where the Governor literally couldn't give the CNC, and Order.

Somehow it seemed to work for them. This discussion was going to be about deploying the PAV Navy to help fight the invasion. I hoped we'd be able to get things moving before the attacker mowed the entire UFS to the ground.

Theresa discussed deployments and expectations and timetables while I more or less ghosted through the meeting on autopilot. I had everything recorded in case I needed to go back and review, but in terms of usefulness, I was barely even eye candy. Finally, Theresa and Admiral Palaz stood and touched fingertips.

Then she did the same with the governor. She looked over to me.

My assistant, Enoki, we'll give you the coordinates for the wormhole portal and instructions for routing through to the Kashmir and Las Cañadas systems.

I nodded, accepting the instructions. When will you need them? Now, Palaz replied.

Oh, when will you be launching? The admiral gave me an impatient look. Now!

I looked at Theresa and grinned. They don't fool around. Admiral Palaz kindly allowed us to come along as supercargo.

They had only one flagship, but it took very little time to add their comms to the Bobiverse-SCUT relay system. Of course, we didn't specifically mention that, simply referring to it as the human backup system. With that addition, the fleet commander would be able to maintain real-time oversight of both battlegroups.

The PAV Navy Yard was stationed in their planet's L2 point. The holotank view was impressive. A little more than a hundred ships, all bio-crewed, the smallest about twice the size of a Titan-class Heaven vessel.

Each vessel had a crew of eight, which I remembered from Phineas and Ferb's notes was some kind of significant number for the PAV We moved out in two groups The first, ours, would head for the Kashmir system and the second battle group would head for Las Cañatas

By now, Manny and Huey connections switched so smoothly when traversing wormholes that the momentary discontinuity was barely more than an eye blink So I wasn't concerned about our Quinny's falling over during the transit The first wormhole transit put us in the Epsilon-Iridani-Kuiper belt, which had become somewhat of a hub

WormNet was officially a network topology, but Bill had been setting up his own backup network just for redundancy It occurred to me not for the first time that Bill's version of replicative drift was probably a magnification of original Bob's engineering mindset and worship of Murphy The path to Kashmir would require two jumps, and to Las Cañadas would require three

We let the Las Cañadas group go first, then transited through The intermediate system was a small red dwarf with a couple of mangy, inhospitable balls of rock for planets and not much in the way of mineable resources Its only value was as a Wormnet relay point.

We moved quickly to the next portal, then grouped up a few clicks away while Bill expanded the portal by remote control.

The fleet commander did an all-ships announcement, then turned to Theresa.

We'll send through a remote to scan the area first. Wouldn't want to gallop into an ambush.

At one end of the control room, one of the PAV crew began muttering into their headset and operating their console. Then a new spec appeared in the holotank as the PAV version of a remote drone separated from the command cruiser and headed for the portal. On the console, a monitor showed the view and telemetry from the drone.

The remote entered the wormhole, established a maser connection with the command ship, and disappeared in a shower of parts. Shit, I said. Shit, said the fleet commander, or something very similar in PAV.

Then he began giving rapid-fire orders. At the same time, what I assumed had to be opposition vessels began pouring through the wormhole. I messaged Bill immediately.

Enemy coming through from Kashmir, can you collapse the portal? I received a nonverbal act, then 200 milliseconds later, the portal collapsed to about a 10-meter diameter. This was enough to let the ships transit but not enough to let them actually get through.

The result was a spray of virtually atomized metals as every enemy ship that couldn't veer off in time became sausage.

A few seconds later, the wormhole shrank more so the enemy couldn't lob missiles through. Meanwhile, the two dozen or so vessels that had gotten through suddenly found themselves up against 50 PAV Navy vessels without any backup.

It was a short and one-sided battle. As the debris from the battle slowly cooled, the fleet commander gazed around at his bridge crew. Analysis?

One crew member spoke up. Missiles, lasers, those things the nosies call busters and plasma cannon. Nothing unexpected.

The missiles are surge-propelled, though very fast. The commander looked at Theresa. Did you arrange the portal collapse?

Theresa glanced at me, then replied, Yes. The human who is responsible for this wormhole network is online. He can re-inflate the wormhole when you desire, subject to a short delay.

Can he inflate it to ten meters, then to full size? I forwarded the request to Bill, and he replied immediately. Two minute delay to charge up the wormhole to begin inflation.

Once I've got it started, though, I can vary inflation speed without penalty. If I start charging early, I can hold the initial inflation until needed. I relayed this to the fleet commander and he nodded thoughtfully.

We will send through a wave of seeker missiles. While the enemy is dealing with that and hopefully distracted, you will inflate the portal the rest of the way and we will flow through. He began giving rapid orders to his crew.

Move all ships to encircle the portal symmetrically in three dimensions. Load all missile bays. Launch full salvo on my command and prepare to follow the missiles through.

The bridge crew scrambled, muttering into headsets and hammering on keyboards. Within minutes, we were ready. The fleet commander gave the command to begin, and Bill said to me, Here goes.

Immediately, the portal expanded. The fleet commander ordered, Weapons, fire full salvo, immediate reload. Madam Sikorski, wait five seconds, then expand to full size.

Nav on my mark. All ships proceed at full acceleration. Fire at will after transit.

Mark.

Everything happened very fast. Well, okay, very fast, if you're bio. I had the privilege of being able to savor the action at replicant speed.

It was still impressive. Our ship followed the attack wave by a few seconds, as befit the command ship, to find a full-on naval battle in progress. Explosions were almost continuous, creating a litter of shrapnel that couldn't be avoided.

No doubt there were plasma cannon and lasers in full operation as well, but there was no way to see them in space unless you were the target. At this stage, the fleet commander could do very little. Things were simply happening too fast, but hopefully still on the script that he'd set out.

I got the impression as well that individual PAV captains had considerably more leeway than would an equivalent human Navy captain.

It took close to 10 minutes for all the interactions to play out, but when it was done, the huller tank showed only friendlies still under power. The PAV had lost 16 ships, and the enemy had lost close to 40. Even allowing for the tactical advantage of controlling the wormhole, that seemed one-sided.

I messaged Bill. This seems like something we should take a close look at. The PAV just kicked someone's ass in their first ever naval battle.

Either whoever is attacking us is at the level of Pakleds, or the Pav are way nastier than we thought. Can you discuss with Will? Bill responded, Yeah, I noticed that too.

I have the recording from your Quinny. We'll go over it.

Meanwhile, the fleet commander had reorganized his ships to fill in the gaps and directed the group inbound for the colony planet.

It was going to be a couple of hours, even at military-level acceleration, so I headed for our quarters for a nap. Once there, I laid the Quinny down and popped into our command center. There I found an active and vocal contingent of Bobs, checking maps, communicating with remote battalions, or just arguing with each other.

And, no surprise, Thor was running the show.

Where's Bill? I said to him. Dunno, he left me in charge.

I guess I've been promoted. Thor gestured to a star map. We're getting eyes into every system, plus establishing liaisons with local military reps, and we're starting to learn some things.

Useful things? Not sure yet. One item, the attacks all seem to be biased to anti-spinwarden.

Galactically? 10-4. It's like they came from that direction and gathered all their troops on that side of every system.

Every time they start to get their ass kicked and call in reinforcements, they always come from anti-spinwarden. Huh. That narrows the search area somewhat, I thought for a moment.

And it seems like a really noobish mistake for a military to make. And anyway, still too big to cover any reasonable time. But, Thor cocked his head dramatically.

If and when we manage to defeat them, it'll tell us which way to look for their home world.

One thing at a time, I guess. I pause to consider. I tend to think they will lurk closer in rather than farther so that they can react quickly.

Being a thousand AU out, it would take them about ten days to bring in reinforcements so they won't park way out there. In fact, Thor interjected, based on observed acceleration capability and the speed with which reinforcements have shown up, we think they're no more than 30 to 40 AU out.

Orbit of Neptune or thereabouts. Still a lot of ground to cover. And they might be moving around.

And if they're cloaking, which they almost certainly are, we won't be able to pick them up on simple fly-by scans. Yep, Bill's spy drones were a good idea but we'll have to get lucky to hit pay dirt and we can't expect to do that in every colony system.

I looked up at the status board. Have any Bobs gotten into the mix? No.

Thor's face showed disgust. A couple have tried but the colonial navies keep warning us off. Say they won't make any effort to distinguish us from hostiles if we get in the way.

Well, they do have a point. I shrugged. I'm going to go look for Bill.

Ciao, Thor replied then turned back to his command board. I sent a text to Bill asking him if he had time for a talk. Instead of an answer, I received an invitation to Bill's pub.

In two hours. It was addressed to the ancients, which meant it was Senior Bob's only. Those were always interesting, although bad as often as good, so I wasn't going to form any conclusions beforehand.

Meanwhile, I had a couple of hours to kill.
