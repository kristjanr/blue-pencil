It was a short text with no follow-up information. We're under attack. I'd been working on the warp drive problem but dropped it immediately and started digging.

The text was from a Bob named Sheldon who was our liaison with the Sasquatch colony. Before I could even formulate a response, I got another text. Attack from Unknown Forces.

This one was from the Bob in the Gamma Pavonis system. Within moments, I'd received several more texts. It appeared to be a coordinated, simultaneous assault on all human-occupied worlds.

But by whom? I sent requests for more information to all texts, then followed up with queries to all the government officials that I had addresses for. Lastly, I sent a text to Will, who would have more and better contacts.

Later, came back from Will. Okay, he was undoubtedly a little busy. Maybe I could find something out on my own.

I quickly sent queries to all the SCUT relay stations in human space for any kind of telemetry.

And got no answer from most of them. A follow-up ping showed that several more had just gone offline. Whoever was attacking human space was taking out strategic targets.

I called Howard. Hey, Bill, I've already seen the news about the attacks, if that's what you're calling about. What's up?

Howard, I don't know if the attackers know about WormNet, but if they take out the satellites, we're screwed. It took decades to build that up in the first place. Do you have any kind of defenses in place?

Negative. I have a bad feeling the Babaverse is underprepared for this. Okay.

I paused and took a quite unnecessary breath. Give me a second to transfer to vert. I need a frame jack to get ahead of this.

I instructed my mani to rack itself, then transferred to my VR. Once there, I cranked up the frame rate to the maximum without losing my environment. Howard?

Here, Bill. Not sure how, since they're destroying all the SCUT relays. I grinned, although Howard couldn't see it.

After the Starfleet war, I quietly implemented a few safeguards. The Bobiverse owns a private SCUT relay station in the Oort cloud of every inhabited system, and most of the uninhabited ones within human space, unregistered, cloaked, and automatic failover, of course.

I hesitated. Truth be told, I wouldn't be surprised if most human colonies have hidden backup systems as well. We might see some failover there in a few moments.

I haven't heard about any of this, although it makes sense. Hold on.

Howard popped into my VR. If we've got reasonably assured comms, we might as well talk in person.

He dropped into a lazy boy. Howard, the Starfleet war involved bobs. They used public

knowledge about the Bobiverse to design their attack strategies. I've had to accept since then that secrecy is necessary. I don't know if the colonies are being attacked by a Bob faction or not.

I paused, a shocking thought making my jaw drop. Or maybe this is Starfleet finally taking a more direct approach to things. Either way, I get your point.

Whoever it is doesn't know about the private relays and you're turning out to be right, Howard sighed. Well, I think we managed a decade and a bit of peace in Starfleet, but now it's back into the dumpster fire. I started to answer just as Will popped in.

I've been getting reports trickling in from the various human navies. Thank the universe for your private relays, Bill. Howard glared at me.

Will knows, but not me. Then he lost the glare and grinned. Just kidding, I get it.

Will spared him a smile before turning back to me. We're trying to get images, but nothing's been close enough and survived yet. One piece of good news anyway.

It's not the others. No death asteroids or giant cylinders. They seem to be using traditional weapons.

Missiles, busters, lasers, and so on. so I'm inclined to think it's a domestic threat of some kind. I'm not sure the others would have bothered with the relays anyway, Will.

They'd just belly up and zap the planets. True, Will turned to Howard. Can we do anything to protect WormNet?

I've been monitoring. So far, no attacks on portals. In any case, even if the attackers destroyed the satellites, it takes about two days for the negative energy to bleed off.

And, I added, as long as the other end is kept open, the wormhole won't evaporate. They have to destroy both sides to get rid of it entirely. You can collapse them quickly, though, right?

Will asked. Yeah, I finally got that figured out. Too late to handle Thoth, of course.

Maybe you should do that right now. We don't want the attackers to use Wormnet to get around.

True. I sent a general announcement over the Wormnet system. Any ships preparing to transit would have two minutes before the portals would be collapsed down to standby status.

Okay, so where are we at? Will continued. We've interdicted wormhole travel for the enemy.

How quickly can you re-inflate a wormhole if we need it? Uh, about two minutes. Good, so we control the interstellar highway.

Will paused, thinking. We don't know who is attacking yet, but we do know that it's not a war of extermination or they'd have simply rammed the planets with a relativistic asteroid or something. What do we know?

Howard asked. Will replied,

Someone is taking out SCUT relay stations and space-based auto factory systems and military targets, of course. A couple of civilian ships have probably been destroyed based on loss of contact, but this appears to be an attempt to cripple, not destroy. And no idea who, said a new voice.

I turned in, surprised to find Garfield in the room. I hadn't even realized he'd popped in. As my gaze continued around, I noted that Bob, ANEC, Theresa, and Bridget had also appeared.

Apparently word was getting around. Hey, Gar, I said. We're still waiting for some kind of ID.

I just received something, Will interjected. From the New Holm Navy, they managed to run an automated scout through the enemy defenses, and it got an image before it was shot down. He popped up a window with a heavily pixelated image.

That's definitely human, I observed. Granted, physics will constrain vessel shapes, but that still looks like something humans would have built.

Running a pattern match, Will said distractedly.

Mmm, nope. There are elements that match multiple ship designs, including ours. No joy.

I just did a survey, Gar said. All 13 colonies are under attack, and they're all getting overwhelmed. How big of a navy do these bad guys have, anyway?

That was a shock. Using 100 ships per target just as a wild-ass guess.

That's 1,300 ships. Who in hell could have hidden that much war production for however many years was required?

Starfleet? Garfield replied. Will shook his head.

I'll grant it's the most likely, but it just doesn't feel right. They wouldn't gain anything at this point, and why would they be attacking colonies? I sighed.

I guess it's time to reactivate the tactical central command VR. Honestly, that thing's getting way more use than I'm really happy with. Garfield snickered just as I snapped my fingers.

We all found ourselves in the tactical center, the same one that had been used to defeat the others in the Battle of Earth and had been the command center for the Starfleet war. It was somewhat updated, technology having improved since I first created it, but anyone involved in the

first battle would have recognized it. Now, I said, information gathering, we need to find out what's going on.

I looked around at the group. Howard, you take the Omicron-squared Eridani colonies and Covenant, Will, the 82 Eridani system colonies and Las Kenatas, Bob, Newholm, Poseidon and Caledonia Nova, Bridget,

New Babylon, Drakon, and Arcadia, Garfield, Aurora, Elysium, and Purgatory. I'll take Kashmir and Great River. Theresa, please update the PAV and check for any observed activity.

ANEC, you'll need to organize a defense for the Quinlan system. We also have two nascent colonies, ANEC replied as people began popping out. They will need protection.

Fortunately, we have recently opened portals at those locations.

He gave me a nod and disappeared, leaving me alone in the command center. Well, I guess I'd better get started, I muttered. I looked up the address for our Kashmir contact and realized in passing that I wouldn't be visiting the archivist anytime soon.

Kashmir was a relatively new colony and an oddball in that it wasn't settled by Will's Earth Diaspora, but by a splinter group from 82 Iridany.

For all our joking about the smell on Takama, it turned out to be the gravity that was the deal-breaker. Some people, even several generations in, just couldn't adapt to a higher G. As soon as a random Bob reported the discovery of a compatible planet with a gravity of 0.92 g, a group from Asgard had laid claim with the UFS and proceeded to settle it.

Seventy-odd years later, they were thriving.

I got an immediate response to my ping and popped over. I found myself in a regular-looking-home-office kind of layout with a stranger facing me from a desk chair. I quickly checked metadata and discovered that this environment was in real.

The stranger was a Bob named Andy, inhabiting a mani specifically designed to not look like Bob. and I was visible to him in AR. Well, okay then.

I quickly built a local environment that matched Andy's office so that we wouldn't get glitches then plunked myself down in my version of his office couch. Hi, Bill, he said.

We've never met but I'm one of your descendants, maybe 15 generations down. Cool. And what is your function here?

Well, I'm the civilian Bobiverse contact, of course. Otherwise, you wouldn't be here. But as a Kashmir citizen, I'm a current affairs journalist named Andy Gleeson.

I nodded. which allows you to be nosy without generating suspicion. Exactly.

Okay, so what do you have on the attacks? Andy settled more comfortably into his chair which adjusted automatically.

I've been in touch with a few contacts in government. They can't get me much.

Kashmir is a second wave colony so it's pretty new. We've started building a military as has everyone since the Starfleet War but we're way behind the

curve. The attackers, whoever they are, have taken out most of our forces and bases. We have a few squadrons

still out there but they're outnumbered, outgunned and don't know what they're up against. Any non-military damage?

No, and my military contact has commented on that. This is a surgical strike, not a full-on assault. No communications?

Nothing. My bet is the attacker will make a statement once they have control of the high ground. Keep me posted, Andy, I said, especially if we get some communications from them.

My next stop was Great River. This world had been colonized mostly by North American First Nations, both Canadian and American, with some Mexican plus Central and South American. They had all emigrated on the Bellerophon after the battle for Earth, so they were able to negotiate for this planet and settle as a group.

When I pinged my contact there, he offered to host me in his VR. I popped over to find myself in what appeared to be a longhouse. The individual sitting across from me, though, was dressed in generic 23rd-century casual.

I checked metadata. Another Bob. Hi, Bill.

I'm Gordon Lightfoot. What, like the Canadian singer? Yeah.

Original Bob really liked him, but for some reason the artist's music died out, so no one gets the reference. I shrugged. None of my business, but it wasn't something I'd have expected from a Bob.

Gordon was probably many generations removed, so I shouldn't be trying to predict behavior. So, Gordon, what can you tell me about the attacks? Ah, well, the Great River Navy seems to be holding them off.

There's been a lot of damage on both sides, but last I looked, it had settled into a stalemate. No communications, though. And no mass destruction?

No. Other than military targets? All right, Gordon, let me know if and when they say something.

I looked around at the longhouse. Bit of a cliché, isn't it? Not as much as you'd think, Bill.

The Great River population has developed a culture that makes a point of living as low-tech as reasonable. They have auto factories and space navies, of course, but no mass media industry, very little consumerism, and no corporations. Wow.

I admit I've never taken the time to study most of the colonies, but that sounds interesting. I frowned. It doesn't seem compatible, though, with having a big enough navy to hold off the attacker.

There's lifestyle, and there's strategic planning, Bill. It's not that the Great River colonists have a chip on their collective shoulder as such, but they've taken the walk-softly philosophy to heart.

Hmm. Can't say I disapprove. We are, after all, the poster boy for over-engineering.

Gordon grinned, then gave me a Boy Scout salute as I stood to leave.
