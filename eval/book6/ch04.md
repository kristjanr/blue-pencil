Today was the day. Twenty heaven vessels floated in a loose gaggle in the Malacca system less than a thousand kilometers from the wormhole that would take us to the PGF. It was somewhat disconcerting, because other than with Day, I had never personally experienced this kind of physical grouping.

It actually felt crowded.

We gathered in VR in Bill's Moot Pub again. This was likely the last time we'd all be together at the same time until the project was complete, if that ever happened.

I made a throat-clearing noise, then said, Okay, guys, it looks like we're ready to go. Everyone has their assigned tasks or routes.

We're going to be out of touch for the duration, unless you take the time to come back and report in. Make sure you drop a buoy at every new location with a note about your next move. Update any buoy you come across with your most up-to-date version of the shared blog.

Store and forward is basically all we have, until and unless we can get some kind of network communications across the PGF network. On that subject, Dae and I will be visiting the archive as first thing and we'll see if we can get access to the PGF communication system.

If so, you'll find out about it when you report in, so that's a good reason to do so.

Elmer raised a hand. How do you suppose they maintain SCUT across wormhole interfaces?

Dae answered before I could. We know that the shepherding satellites, besides keeping the wormhole supplied with a negative energy scaffolding, also maintain a maser connection with their counterparts on the other end.

Kind of a WAN connection between local SCUT networks.

Seems to me that would introduce some latency, Elmer grumbled. Maybe, and throughput limits, I replied. Some manis might not be viable in the PGF.

I find it hard to believe we're more advanced than they are, Calvin interjected. You sure about this? We're not sure about anything, Calvin, I said.

But don't forget, our entire human bubble is a single sphere with everything in it within range of one or more SCUT relay stations.

We don't have to worry about relaying through the wormholes. The PGF, on the other hand, stretches around an arc of about 120,000 light-years, and that's just the hubs on the ring at 26,000 light-years radius.

You also have all the radial spokes and all the local hops to individual systems and any other rings farther in or out. Some of the member systems might be within 25 light-years of each other and able to maintain direct SCUT connections, But I'm betting that's the exception.

Or they're at least limited to individual species territories. Wormhole travel creates a whole new rulebook. I noticed Day frowning and made a note to talk to him about it later.

Day didn't ever get that look on his face for nothing. I continued after a moment. For purposes of this project, we'll be numbering the rings outward from zero, starting with the antimatter fountain and the spokes spinward from zero.

The hub at the spoke and ring intersection will be hub zero, and intermediate hubs will count upward from there until the next spoke. What about the Capitals Hub, Hub Zero? It's 4-4-0 now, let's just call it the Capital Hub, no point in confusing things, and if Star Trek can change cannons, so can we.

I looked around, surprisingly, no one commented.

All right then, Elmer said after several mils of silence. If there's nothing else, let's get this show on the road. The room erupted in spontaneous cheering, fist pumps, and shouts of, yeah, no surprise, these were Bobs, and this was exploring.

Day and I had taken on the task of negotiating with the Archivist for access to the PGF Communications Backbone. We didn't feel confident, though. Before the entire Federation left for the Large Magellanic Cloud 2,000 years ago, the PGF administration seemed to have taken the time to lock down what the Archivist could and could not talk about with non-member species.

I thought it was a little odd, and Day thought it was a lot odd, but we hadn't had the opportunity to pursue the subject on our first meeting with the Archivist. The various Bobs were flying through the wormhole at two-minute intervals to allow us to space ourselves out. We already knew it was safe for multiple ships to fly through at the same time, because Dae and I had done it many times when we first explored the PGF network, but we didn't want pile-ups from multiple impatient Bobs.

Finally, it was our turn. Dae and I popped through the wormhole and found ourselves outside the Roanokean system.

In the holotank, we could see several Heaven vessels ahead of us as other Bobs headed for the next wormhole leading to the PGF network. We followed the queue, keeping an orderly formation. Four hops later, we were in the PGF Capital system

We were now a registered vessel type, so we didn't get more than a routine challenge from traffic control I invited Day over and set out the scripting for our upcoming conversation Nothing in here about shooting up the place, Day said

That's good That's what the new script is for I don't need the aggravation

We batted a few ideas around, but really, we'd been working on this for a couple of years There wasn't much room left for improvement Girding my virtual loins, I pressed Execute

You are number 114,201 in the queue, the archivist said, please hold. Yeah, I got your please hold right here, I muttered. Microphone's off, right?

Dae said. I snorted, yes. The script will provide all the I.O.

We can carp all we want in the background. Essentially, we had set up an avatar of a Bob to ask questions. It had a predefined list and couldn't be derailed or sidetracked, and it wouldn't get annoyed by the constant queue announcements.

We sat back and listened to the exchange, frame-jacked down to half-speed to filter out the delays. Can we have access to the PGF network communications system? The avatar asked.

Access requires membership in the PGF, the archivist replied. How can we get membership?

An application must be submitted to the Ministry of External Affairs. Is that ministry currently active? All ministry staff are on indefinite leave.

Are they on their way out of the galaxy? That information is not available. Took a little longer than usual to get there this time, Dae said to me as the exchange continued.

Is there a procedure for applying for membership in the absence of the ministry staff? Yes. Application may be made through the ministry avatar.

Is the ministry avatar an AI? Yes. And they can approve membership?

No. But you said applications could be made to the ministry avatar.

Applications can be made. Ministry staff are still required for membership approval. Hell's bells, I muttered.

Phrasing is everything, Dae replied I leaned forward and flipped a switch Okay, this is going to go nowhere

I'm moving to branch B Our avatar changed the subject Can privately owned drones or satellites be left in proximity to wormholes?

Traffic rules require that no vessel may take up a position between 85 degrees north and 85 degrees south with a radial velocity of less than 1,000 kilometers per hour for a radius of 1,000 kilometers

Huh, I said I checked the conversion to human values Yep, looks correct

What about within five degrees of the poles? our avatar asked.

No vessel may approach or depart a wormhole within five degrees of a pole. This is a safety requirement to preclude any interaction with portal satellites.

Well, that makes sense, I suppose, I said. You don't want someone barreling into one of the shepherding satellites at speed?

Can a privately owned drone take up a stationary position within the five-degree zone? Yes, with the proviso that it must remain a minimum of five hundred meters from the portal satellite. Bingo, I exclaimed.

We can set up our own network. Mazer com drones. Dae reached down and paused the Bob avatar.

That's a lot of drones, icky. Two per wormhole, one on each end?

Yeah, I'd have preferred to use the PGF communication network, but that's not in the cards. I pressed resume on the avatar. Are there any limitations on such private drone installations?

They must be registered with the network authority, or they will be cleared during periodic maintenance checks. How do we register? The first step is to acquire PGF membership.

Oh, hell! I yelled. I feel like I'm in the movie Brazil, Dae muttered.

I'm going to try one last thing, I said and typed quickly.

Is there a process for a non-member to be able to acquire the ability to communicate across the wormhole network in a situation like the current one where membership cannot be acquired due to lack of staff?

Wow, that's a mouthful, Dae said. I'm desperate. One moment, the archivist replied.

Wait, what? I exclaimed. That's never happened before.

There is a provision for a temporary approval, essentially a provisional membership. It lasts two standard years and must be renewed upon expiry, until and unless ministry staff are able to execute their duty.

I typed quickly.

Why did you request us to wait one moment? our avatar asked. That's never happened to us before.

The relevant statute is an emergency measure implemented approximately 6,000 years ago during the Great Upheaval and has not been invoked since. I had to access deep archives for details. What was the great upheaval?

That information is not available. Will it be available once we achieve provisional status? Yes.

There will be increased but not total access to PGF archives. Well, okay, then. Please provide information on how to apply for provisional membership.
