I was happy to have handed off the Hulk to Bill. While it was cool as hell to find an alien derelict, it was also pretty obvious that we wouldn't be emulating Herschel and Neil by repairing and recommissioning it. That baby was never going to fly again.

Bill would figure it out. He was the Bobiverse's chief nerd. Where to, Kal?

Goku asked. I say let's try a slightly different goal, Gok. Let's ride each spoke outbound as far as possible to see how far outside the galaxy they take us, just for mapping.

Sounds good to me, let's do it. This would take a while, of course. We'd be moving spinward along ring 6, which by definition would cover the greatest distance.

Whether that meant more jumps or just more distance between jumps was yet to be determined. We jumped back to hub 630 and then began rechecking all the gate addresses until we found the portal that would take us spinward. A quick acceleration and we were through.

The next hub wasn't a radial hub but appeared to be midway between radial spokes 3 and 4. It put us just outside the norma or outer arm of the Milky Way. This was a fairly sparse arm, and the hub reflected that, with only a dozen or so portals available.

A check of destination addresses didn't show anything memorable.

There was only one long-distance portal, which was obviously the next spinward hub. Based on the location, that was also obviously the radial hub for Spoke 4. Again, transit time to the portal was negligible with the power of the Titan hulls.

This next hub was considerably better populated, although not for any obvious reason.

We were still on the outside of the outer arm, but we were also getting close to the Centaurus arm.

I did the usual querying and dismissed most of the portals as local hops. That left the portal we'd just exited and portals to spinward, outbound, inbound, and one other.

Goku, look at the addressing on this one. He glanced at it. Then, that can't be right.

It's completely outside the galaxy. Yeah, it's... I frame-jacked momentarily to do a deep dive on our astronomical database.

As near as I can tell, it's NGC-6934. A globular cluster? He exclaimed.

Uh-huh. Want a visit? Oh, hell yes!

Goku immediately began reorienting on the new portal. Hold on, I said, let's do this right. I dropped a buoy with all our collected historical data plus an entry detailing what we were about to do.

In case they need to retrieve our bodies, I commented. How bad is the radiation likely to be, he mused. Ick and Day visited Sagittarius A-Star.

NGC-69-34 isn't going to be anywhere near that level, we're good. I oriented myself to parallel him. Let's do this.

We accelerated through the portal, then looked around in shock.

Holy shit, Goku exclaimed. Holy shit indeed, I echoed. Our portal was fairly close to a small white dwarf star, probably because any farther out and it would get stripped away.

Between our vector away from the portal and the proper motions of the local stars, we were seeing the stars move in real time.

Not much, of course, but enough to be detectable with our instruments over a couple of minutes. We were staring in awe at the stellar vista when Guppy popped into the common VR. Alert, Singularity Detected

What? I exclaimed Where?

A red circle superimposed itself on the view It, or the stars around it, were visibly changing position and it had that visual distortion that had long since come to be associated with black holes

How close, Guppy? I asked Less than 100 AU, Goku volunteered before Guppy could reply

Not a supermassive black hole, but still black and holy enough to wreck your weekend And close enough to disrupt this system, I replied Which would mean no ride home

I think we should leave Amen to that. Timely departure from vicinity is highly recommended.

Okay, even Guppy was freaked out. That just capped it. We turned as one, pointed ourselves at our portal, and hit the gas for all we were worth.

In seconds, we were back in our galaxy. It's kind of too bad. I mused.

In maybe a couple of years, that portal will be swallowed, and there won't be any more access. I think we should put a drone through to record things. Have one on this side to receive the reports on telemetry, right up until the last minute.

Hey, here's a question. Does gravity come through a wormhole? If the other end gets swallowed,

What happens to this end? You want to be around for that? Probably not.

Let's go with the drone idea. Works for me. We'll let Bill know about it as well.

And ask those questions. Just for kicks. Without another word, we began modifying a couple of drones to act as scientific instruments.
