The blat of the air horn was met with a low growl from the assembled audience. It had already been made clear to me that the mood of this moot was considerably different from the last one. The news was out about Medeiros, and I had posted both his monologue and his manifesto up on active walls beforehand.

Every Bob, by definition, remembered Bob 1's first encounter with Medeiros.

The dismissiveness and casual arrogance of the Brazilian replicant had left a bad taste. In addition, many Bobs had the memory, direct or indirect, of the battles for 82 Eridani, and no one was going to let this pass. This was now personal.

We still need, I began, but I was immediately cut off. I have two auto factories ready. I already started producing busters.

Beat Aurora in two days. Kill the motherfucker a lot. I gave the air horn three short blasts to get control of the floor.

Fortunately, original Bob was a polite individual, even when irate. Everyone quieted immediately.

I am happy to see this newfound desire to help out the colonies, and I really don't care if it comes from a place of wanting to crush Medeiros. I'll take the win, and the colonies will take the help. Up to a point.

Apparently they don't want us interfering with the local navies and acting like vigilantes. I was interrupted by a solid wall of boo and not the good-natured booing I usually got. This was a someone's-gonna-die level of anger.

I didn't say we're going to obey, I yelled, and the crowd went silent, except for a few chuckles and yips. We don't have to fly in formation to help. Medeiros appears to have set up reserves in the outer system of each colony and doesn't commit more than he needs to at any time.

This is a sound psychological ploy, since it gives the defenders the impression he has an infinite supply of attackers. He doesn't, of course, but we don't know what he does have and therefore have to stick to a primarily defensive strategy.

I paused for the inevitable audience participation, then continued. So I'm going to suggest that we try to locate and neutralize Medeiros' out-system reserves. That will help take the pressure off the Colonial Navies, and they don't have the assets to do that themselves, so we won't be getting in their way.

The crowd immediately broke into groups, gesticulating and arguing. I looked around and realized that the meeting had just ended, unless I had something else of interest to offer, which I didn't.

I glanced over at Will and he laughed, then waved me over and pointed to the pub. I shook my head and texted an invitation to him and the other ancients, to my home VR. I wasn't going to be able to get the crowd's attention long enough to adjourn the moot, so I left the moot VR running and the pub open

There are things we need to talk about, Will said to the group once we settled into our favorite furniture First, the attacks were coordinated to the minute The ships casually entered systems in ones and twos on seemingly innocent and legitimate errands

Then all of them attacked at once If this had been Starfleet or one of the colonies, I wouldn't have considered this significant, but with Medeiros, it brings up some issues Garfield responded in a dry tone, uh-huh, looks like Medeiros has SCUT, probably not a surprise, if he's been skulking around the UFS for more than a couple of decades, he'll have been able to pick up a lot just from monitoring the public feeds, and since those mystery attacks were Medeiros, he may have been extracting technology from his victims, so we're going to be fighting on an even playing field, tech-wise, Bob said, afraid so, Garfield replied, Spikes, he's probably picked up, possibly long-range SUDDAR,

We haven't published anything about cloaking, of course, but since we got that from Medeiros in the first place, but wormholes are so recent that he may not have picked up on that before he started his deployment, I said. Will nodded. It would have taken decades to get all the assets to the proper locations the old-fashioned way.

At that point, he'd be pretty much committed. If he picked up on the WormNet news, the only choice he'd have is go-no-go. He chose go.

And then there's warp drives, I added. I haven't even mentioned them to the Bobiverse at large. Not that they're useful for much.

Yet.

So we have an advantage in inter-system mobility, Will said. We can move our assets around quickly, except we don't have much in the way of war assets that aren't tied to a specific colony, and we don't know where to start anyway.

I zoomed the 3D local space map, which had been updated based on everyone's reports.

We've got reports of attacks at all colonies. Newholm is faring the best since they've had a full-on space navy since day one. The 82 Eridani system is almost as well off since they began building a military presence after the Medeiros attack when Mack was taken out.

Will pointed to several spots on the map. Vulcan, Poseidon, Sasquatch, Drakon's systems are relatively weak and Kashmir and Las Kenyadas are probably all but defenseless except for the PAV fleet. Do we have anything we can deploy?

I've got a dozen Titan-class Heaven vessels around Ultima Thule, I replied, but they're not military, just overpowered civilian ships. Very overpowered, Garfield added, to the point where I think a lot of biogovernments would be deeply concerned if they knew the full ship specs. Is there any danger to Ultima Thule?

Bob, asked. Doubt it.

Of all the things I've been tight-lipped about, the existence and location of Ultima Thule is one of the tightest. It's our ultimate backup and archive site. It's so far out beyond Epsilon Eridani's Heliopause that it would take centuries to locate by a blind search, even with long-range SUDAR.

I have to admit, though, I'm a little surprised Medeiros didn't attack Ragnarok, Garfield said. He has such a hard-on for the Bobiverse, you'd think he'd make a point of hitting one of our installations. I tapped my chin and thought, reasonable.

Except, Epsilon Eridani gets very little actual traffic in the normal course of affairs, and I would have noticed if a bunch of miscellaneous ships started spontaneously showing up.

Medeiros has clearly been planning this for a long time, and I don't think he's taking anything at all for granted.

If we were to kick his ass for a fifth time, Garfield laughed, yeah, I think he would self-destruct just out of shame.

At that moment, I got a ping from ANEC. He'd sent regrets on the meeting invitation, so I was momentarily nonplussed, but I immediately invited him into my VR.

Greetings, Bill. Please assume standard complaint about beer as having been made. I must talk to you about the situation with Medeiros.

Of course, Anik, have a seat. Anik shuffled up to a beanbag chair and dropped into it with a sigh. He seemed to like them.

As the PAV have offered military support, Anik began, I feel there cannot help but be an expectation that the Quinlans would follow. Anik, we can't really—Anik held up a hand. It's okay, Bill.

It is not an unreasonable expectation. given what your species has done for us. Nevertheless, he hesitated.

Although we nominally have a navy, I have been careful not to develop technology or capabilities that are even reminiscent of a military theme.

Quinlans slip too easily into combat mode and slip out less easily. He stopped and eyed me. Literally, with one eye.

However, we can offer manufacturing capability. That is one of a few areas where our technology outpaces yours. I estimate that one of our auto factories can outproduce one of yours by a factor of between five and eight, depending on complexity of product.

I hereby offer our manufacturing capability for the duration of the emergency. Our colonies can get by on their local equipment for the moment.

Well, thank you, ANEC. The colonial navies would love a steady supply of missiles and busters, and the Bobiverse would love to ramp up on long-range survey drones.

ANEC cocked his head. That seems odd. For scouting the enemy, perhaps?

Exactly. We have to find them in the outer system of every colony with no guarantee that they'll all follow the same pattern.

In fact, given my understanding that this Medeiros is a career military officer, Anik replied, It would be gross incompetence to be that predictable.

Uh-huh. So look, I'll forward the plans for all three units to you, and whatever you can produce, we can use.

And thank you. You are welcome. Anik stood, and with no further ado, disappeared.
