We were back out to the first alien hub, the one we'd informally identified as 2-9-0. We'd agreed that the scrapyard was something that would require a team to explore, preferably a team that didn't include us. Neither of us could explain it, but the eerie image of all those hulks floating around, frozen and forgotten, creeped us both out.

We couldn't put our fingers on anything specific, it just felt haunted. So, we decided it was time to check out the second portal, if only to get our minds off the graveyard.

I ordered a drone through for the requisite pictures and accepted the feed as it came in. With Goku leaning over my shoulder, I ran the stellar positioning program.

When the results popped up, I stared, my jaw in danger of coming unhinged. Holy, exclaimed Goku. Shit, I added.

The other end was, according to the app, almost on top of Nemesis. Or maybe more accurately, Nemesis was almost on top of the other end. The other end of our wormhole was at the edge of the galaxy and maybe 5,000 light years from Nemesis.

Why can't we see it? I asked. We're facing the portal from the wrong direction, but the drone would have gotten a picture.

Pull up the images.

I dissolved my VR into the Stellarium mode and put up the full spherical image from our drone. Goku and I floated in the center, disembodied. I looked in the direction opposite galactic center and saw...

nothing. Well, not nothing, exactly. The halo of stars.

Almost a small galactic core in itself that encircled Nemesis was clearly visible, but not the supermassive black hole. Well, that's disappointing, I said. The radius of the black hole would be about 600 million kilometers, Goku observed.

That's less than the orbit of Jupiter. You really think we're going to see it from 5,000 light years? Well, no, but I was thinking there might be some distortion, or it might be warping the galactic disk or something.

Most of the galactic disk is behind us in this view. I think we're going to have to magnify if you want to be properly awestruck. The image doesn't have enough pixel depth, I replied.

We're gonna have to go through. Like hell, get the drone to do it! I snickered, but complied.

A few seconds later, we had an image magnified a few thousand times in the direction of Nemesis. At this magnification, it was just possible to make out a halo of stars, like a globular cluster with a hole in the middle. I don't think it's going to get any better than that, I said.

You sure you don't want to go through and look for ourselves? Caution, I said, and caution, I meant. You can volunteer, though.

I ignored the job and we let the silence grow for a few moments. This can't be coincidence, I said. You mean the location of the other end?

Uh-huh. A 45,000 light-year jump straight toward Nemesis with no other obvious reason for the portal to be there. They did this purposely.

They being the unknown aliens, obviously. No way the PGF did this. The PGF is more than 22,000 years old, so the aliens would have had to construct this before then.

What, they couldn't see Nemesis coming? Goku sighed. Fair enough.

Hey, maybe they set up this forward observation point to study Nemesis, realized what he was going to do and bugged out themselves. I paused, thought about that. Not the worst idea.

Then the PGF formed, discovered Nemesis themselves and did the same. I'm noticing a pattern. Let's go talk to Bill.

And hand it off to him. I'm really starting to regret having come back to human space. I nodded.

You and me both.
