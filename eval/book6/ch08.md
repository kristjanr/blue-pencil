I had to admit to being a bit nervous. I'd never actually met a Pav face-to-face, but I had a lot of feedback from the various Bobs who had. If the Quinlans were overly short-tempered, the Pav were hair-trigger belligerent.

On the other hand, the Pav didn't like humans, so their behavior toward another species like the Quinlans might be more mellow. I hoped so for Theresa's sake, and mine for that matter.

She had unilaterally decided to set up diplomatic relations between the Quinlans and the Pav, and in true Theresa style, she negotiated like a steamroller. She couldn't be deflected, stalled, distracted, bullshitted, or brushed off, as several officials had by now discovered, to their dismay. Teresa received approval from both the provisional Quinlan government and from the PAV, but if asked, I couldn't swear that they hadn't just gotten tired of arguing with her, which meant if we were invited to dinner, I would be sure to check if we were guests or the main course.

Yes, we. I had agreed to go with Teresa nominally as her assistant, and no, I wasn't going to show up in human form. That would have guaranteed me the less pleasant version of the dinner invitation.

Of course, I doubted Manny's would cook up well anyway. I had picked up a couple of Quinneys, the Quinlan form of Manny from Heaven's River, then we'd headed for new PAV in one of the new official Quinlan Navy frigates. With WormNet, it was now far easier to get around to the point where many Bobs were doing a lot less remote connecting.

It didn't help that SCUT connection reliability was becoming an issue lately. Howard's new Hueys were a resounding success, and humanity was making great use of them and swamping the network. We'd originally designed the relays with what we thought was massive overcapacity,

but no one had planned on the entire human race coming on board with full sensory connections.

The Bobiverse had been quietly building our own private network since the Starfleet war. No humans or ex-humans allowed, but it was a work in progress, and meanwhile, Theresa and I would have to be careful that we didn't run into a laggy public internet.

I wasn't actually sure if the Pav knew that we had a wormhole set up outside their system. Technically, it was far enough out that they couldn't legally require us to remove it, but I wasn't all that sure the Pav would be worried about legalities.

They tended to shoot from the hip. At the moment, the Quinlan ship was halfway between the portal and the planet of New Pav, so our Quinneys were lying down in our stateroom while Theresa and I relaxed in my VR, in human form. Theresa was sampling a scan of one of Howard's newest beers.

She sipped, eyed it with a frown, then nodded.

Acceptable. I'll want to try it in real, as well.

I doubt you'll notice a difference, I replied. It's been an arms race between the VR people and the biologists like Bridget trying to make Mannies as faithful as possible to biological responses. Nowadays, it can be hard to even remember which environment you're in.

Have you made a Pav, Manny, yet? I shook my head. Bridget suggested I get some deep scans of some Pav while we're there, if I can do it discreetly.

She was fascinated by your revelation about Quinlan hearing. Howard says she's on a crusade now to find all the sensory differences between species. At that moment a call came in from New Pav.

We both immediately changed to Quinlan form. As Theresa's nominal assistant, I picked up. Good day.

This is the butters of New Pav External Affairs. Is this the Sikorsky party I'm speaking to? It is.

I am her assistant. Taz cut me off. I will speak to Teresa Sikorski now.

With an expression of mock surprise, I pushed the connection window over to Teresa. Path Society was somewhat matriarchal, and Taz apparently considered me beneath contempt.

Teresa touched the window and said,

"'This is she. Ah, Mrs. Sikorsky, the new power of government welcomes you as the representative of the Quinlan race.

We hope we can establish friendly relations and possibly even some trade agreements. Do you have any Quinlan staff with you that we will need to accommodate?' Teresa and I exchanged smiles. The emphasis had been subtle but unmistakable.

Humans were not invited.

"'My assistant, Enochi Fungi, will be accompanying me,' she replied. Welp, looked like I was back on the clock. We came down in a standard lander rather than as cargo in a drone.

Theresa and I had agreed that our best strategy would be to act as much like bios as possible. In fact, unless directly questioned, we wouldn't even mention that we were running Quinny's. I just hoped some random PAV didn't try to eat us.

It wouldn't end well for anyone. But at least for now, it didn't look like it would be a problem. We were ushered into a ground vehicle, which took us to the local government building.

I wasn't clear on the PAV governmental system, but I remember from Phineas and Ferb's notes that it was considerably more fluid than anything humans would have tolerated. The PAV representative, Pazila Behir, met with us in an office in the building. The translation software rendered her title as Planetary Governor, which I could tell was gratifying to Theresa.

Of course, Theresa had been part of the academic structure of Heaven's River all her life, so titles and rituals were a source of reassurance and comfort for her. For me, such things had always been annoying time-wasters.

Behir performed a small bow, then extended fingers to each of us in turn, and we touched the tips of our fingers to hers. It was a new social custom developed in the last 30 to 50 local years. No one at our end was sure how or why, and an ex-human sociologist had asked me to make discreet inquiries about it if the opportunity permitted.

I treated myself to a small internal smile. Sociology was one of many fields of study that were benefiting greatly from post-life replication. Being effectively immortal meant that you could personally run a study over several biogenerations and still be around to publish the results.

But, Behir was talking, I quickly rewound and caught myself up. Welcome to New Pave. We have been fascinated by the story of the Quinlans and have been following your progress for years.

You've been fortunate, I think, in your relationship with the nosies, far more than we— Ah, excuse me. Nosies?

Theresa said. Humans, sorry, it's local slang, always sticking their noses into others' business. Theresa frowned.

But they freed us from virtual captivity, and my understanding is that they saved the Pave race from extinction. Behir waved dismissively. No one questions their actions, just their motivations.

They're always at you, trying to help. Behir made a gesture with her hands that my translator rendered as making air quotes.

And claiming they want nothing in return. And this is bad? Behir gave Theresa a small smile equivalent.

The most trustworthy transactions are value for value. If you demand a reasonable wage for your effort, I can expect you will give your best work.

If you ask nothing, I can have no expectation of the quality of your product, but am expected to accept the result. In our society, offering to do something for free is an

insult. It says that I have nothing of value and must take whatever you give me. Theresa looked

sideways at me and I tried to keep my face neutral. Of course, I was wearing a Quinlan face and Behir had no translation software to help her interpret Quinlan facial expressions.

Still, better not to give away anything. I did get an intercom comment from Theresa, though.

Well, that's interesting. She was right. Apparently, we'd been completely missing the point of the path's dislike for humans.

I hope we can arrive at equitable value for value, Theresa said to Beheer, instinctively picking up the proper phrasing. Beheer gave her another small bow.

We will meet tomorrow if that is convenient and begin a dialogue. Until then. Until then.

We were ushered out and shown to a sizable apartment in the building. We set up sleeping arrangements, parked our Quinny's, and returned to VR.
