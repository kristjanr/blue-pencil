I received an invitation from Professor Stephen Gilligan on behalf of the O'Neill Corporation, which immediately got me excited. They were the company that was arranging to build megastructures based on advances gleaned from the Quinlans and ANEC. The technology trading over the last decade had been profitable for both sides, of course, and no one was complaining.

The Quinlans had received SURGE, SUDAR, and SCUT tech, and more recently were given access to WormNet. Humanity, in turn, received tech relating to artificial intelligence which the Skippies had mostly been interested in.

Most of the rest of humanity seemed happy enough with AMIs. Of course, a couple of centuries of horror movies and books about AI apocalypses might have biased humanity against the idea in general. Certainly, ANEC wasn't being invited to any events in human space.

I popped into the address I had been given, which I assumed was Steven's VR, but when I arrived, I found myself on what appeared to be the bridge of a human-occupied spaceship. Steven was talking to a couple of people I didn't recognize. They were wearing booties with a version of the VAST system that had been

Standard on roamers for centuries. It wasn't gravity, but it was better than Velcro booties like in 2001 A Space Odyssey. Sadly, no form of actual artificial gravity had yet been developed.

On the other side of the group, a viewport showed what could only be an O'Neill cylinder floating in space with a sterile rocky planet in the background. Stephen looked up. Ah, well, good to see you.

We're just getting ready to officially power up the A.C. Clark. He gestured expansively toward the viewport and presumably the megastructure.

I looked around me. Is this augmented reality? Yes, in fact, you and I are holograms right now.

As a first attempt, it was considerably less ambitious. With a length of 20 kilometers and a diameter of 10, it had a Usable Inner Surface of just over 628 square kilometers.

I knew because I'd done the reading. The builders were aiming for a stable population density of a thousand people per square kilometer, although the initial level would be less than half that. Of course, there was greenery everywhere.

This was a landscape designed from the ground up with an eye to livability over the long term. Buildings were low and tended to cluster in villages of a sort which were well separated. There was very little in the way of straight lines.
