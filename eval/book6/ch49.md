I popped into the command center to find a whirlwind of activity. It took several seconds to spot Thor, but I couldn't see Will, who I had, for some reason, expected to be here.

I marched over, planted myself, and waited for Thor to have a mill for me. He turned, gave me a nod, but continued his discussion with several other Bobs. It seemed to be about scouting missions, but there was too much detail I wasn't up on.

That felt odd. Normally, I was in the thick of things, but this time out, with me concentrating on the warp drive development, Thor seemed to have taken over. Finally, he turned to face me.

What's up, Bill? Just need an update, Thor. Okay, well, we've got Bobs running scouting drones and

all 13 colonies. Six drones have been pursued and shot down, which tells us that Medeiros has scouts and may have better long-range SUDDAR than us for detecting cloaked units. We've spotted a couple of concentrations of Medeiros ships, but we keep getting blown up as soon as they notice us, and we don't have the assets to go in and clean them out anyway.

The colonial navies? Too busy fending them off in the inner system. I've reported our findings a couple of times, and most responses are not polite.

Well, shit, I muttered. This was not looking good. We didn't have a technological advantage anymore.

We certainly didn't have a numerical advantage. And Medeiros seemed to have strategy all sewn up. I pinged Will, invited him over, and he popped in right away.

Hey, Will, you look kind of down. You're no bundle of joy yourself. You just came from the command center?

Yeah. Got an update from Thor. It's not looking great.

This is down to a war of attrition, I think, and Medeiros overprepared this time. Will nodded, then looked around. So what are you working on?

I sighed and made a vague gesture. I'm not much help with the war itself, I'm more of a logistics person, so I've handed real-time management over to Thor, who seems to revel in it. Meanwhile, I'm continuing to work on the warp drive, and the nemesis problem in the longer term, and a lot of moping around, honestly.

At that moment, we got a message from Thor. Important updates, and not the good kind. Oh, crud, I said.

I glanced at Will.

Shall we?

Please tell me you're kidding, I said a short time later, rubbing my forehead. Afraid not, Bill, Thor pointed at the big board where another colony was flashing red. Elysium has just announced that they are ceding the high ground and pulling back to defend their planets.

So he's got four of thirteen now. Damn it! And no reason to believe it's going to stop.

Thor gave me a piercing look. Medeiros isn't spreading himself thinner. He's not stretching his supply lines.

He's not doing any of the things in traditional wars that lead to slower and slower gains. The attack force for each colony was designed to be independent, given that he didn't know about the wormholes or didn't expect to be able to use them.

What I hear you're not quite saying is that we can expect most of the rest of the battlegrounds to go the same way. 10-4. Of course, the easiest ones fell first and the toughest colonies will last the longest, but at some point, he will be able to just blockade the last survivors and bring in his assets from other systems, maybe quickly, if he gets control of any portals.

That won't happen. He can destroy the satellites, he can interdict the portals, but he can't use them without Howard's or my cooperation.

Sure, Thor said. Still, as I said before, this is now a war of attrition and we'll lose in the long term.

I thought for a moment, then felt a lightbulb go on.

In situations like this, what would Spock do? Will interjected. Change the parameters.

More of a Kirk thing. You mean cheat? Will said.

I laughed. No cheating in real war. But maybe I can change the rules a bit.

I gave Thor a wave and nodded to Will. Can I go talk to Garfield?

I returned to Ultima Thule and invited Garfield in for a discussion. He showed up immediately, looking every bit as morose as I'd been feeling up to a few mils ago.

Garfield hadn't talked to Thor, but even so, he knew intuitively that things weren't going well. The problem at root was that Medeiros seemed to have an unlimited

supply of ships. Whenever it seemed like we'd knocked him back in any location, more Medeiri would show up, blasting into the system at high G.

He didn't have wormholes. We'd have detected the microwave halo if he'd opened one, which meant he had reserves sitting in the outer system, just waiting to be called in.

Why not just send in everyone, I said, repeating the common complaint. We have to find those reserves and knock them out, Garfield replied. I just can't see how.

We've got our hands full, as is. I stared into space, frowning. Wormnet should be giving us an advantage.

It would, if we could clean out one system and move the units somewhere else. Local navies won't leave their them, wait for them to leave, then bam.

I think that's another part of the reserves strategy. It paralyzes us, prevents us from moving resources around. I sighed and sat down.

Which brings up why I gathered you here. We need an edge. A technological one?

I looked up at Gar. Sure, that seems to be what it's going to take. That or a secret golden army we can call up at the last minute.

You know, Bill, I keep thinking about the warp drive variants that produced suboptimal results, at least as warp drives. Is there some way we can use them? Exactly my thought.

and the reason for this discussion, I thought for a moment. There was the exploding variant, which would make a great missile, but let's face it, traditional missiles with traditional explosives do the job just as well, which is not very in this case because Medeiros has SUDDAR. He's matching us, technology for technology.

Bill, did we ever test if SUDDAR could see an approaching warp drive? Hmm. No.

And you're right, it could be valuable. I still have the test rig. Shall we do a quickie test?

I'd love to. It took 20 minutes to get my automated minions to prep the No. 5 warp drive test unit.

When it was ready, Gar said, Okay, how do we want to do this? Well, a three-minute burn will put the unit just outside of long-range SUDDAR.

We'll send it out that-away for one minute, then two, three, four, and five minutes, then have it reverse course and fly back the same distance. We'll keep our SUDDAR on full power and see if we can detect anything on each lap. Let's do this.

I programmed in the flight plan, then hit execute, and we watched the holotank. The test unit disappeared, then appeared a minute later, 42 light minutes downrange. It sat for five seconds, per instructions, then disappeared again.

One minute later, it was back at the original launch point. We continued to watch as the unit went through the increasingly longer cycles. On the four- and five-minute tests, we couldn't see the unit appear at the far end, as expected, but each time, it returned right on schedule.

It doesn't register when it's in warp, I said, aw in my voice. This is like the ultimate stealth combined with speed. A warship could come in undetected, whoop your butt while you were still figuring out what was happening, and disappear again before you could react.

If only we had a couple of years to design that warship, Gar replied acerbically. Yeah, and we still have the immediate problem that the more mass we put in for explosives, the slower it'll go. Oh, yeah.

15% over light speed would still work technically, but I'd like to be able to lob these from farther away. Uh-huh. Hey, I frowned.

How much of the design of units 3 and 5 overlap? Garfield gave me a side-eye. The explody ones?

Hold on. He popped up the schematics and we examined them. About 60, 65% identical, I finally said.

And that includes the emitters, which are the bulkiest parts. I paused for a moment and thought. Come to think of it, it's practically guaranteed that there would be overlap since all of our designs are based on the Dutchman.

What are you thinking, Bill? Build all the circuitry in for Units 3 and 5, fly there using the Unit 5 circuitry, then switch to the Unit 3 circuitry, and make a big explosion. Garfield finished grinning.

Time for some ad hoc engineering.
