Goku and I had elected to try and get as far from the center of the galaxy as possible, on the theory that network segments closer to the Federation capital would be more complete. We'd headed to Spoke 3 and started querying wormholes. It didn't take long to find one with a destination address outbound from our location, about 11,000 light-years outbound.

That tracked with what Icky had said about the network layout. Rings were positioned about every 12,000 light-years outward, give or take.

We dropped a buoy with our stated destination, then flew on through. We came out about 10 AU from Red Dwarf, with nothing immediately around us. A quick scan of the system showed a couple of small rocky planets in close, but nothing much else.

Definitely not an inhabited system. But what a view. Messier 56 was a globular star cluster, not quite bright enough to be naked-eye visible from Earth and perhaps a quarter the size of the moon, but from this system, it was a next-door neighbor, easily visible and huge.

I wouldn't be surprised if a human could read by its light, assuming we had a human on board and a place to put them.

We should have stocked some manis, I muttered, just to test the view from real eyeballs.

Yeah, Goku replied, I have a feeling we'll be wishing that a lot on this tour. There were three other wormholes in the system. When queried, they connected to a spinward, anti-spinward, and outward address.

We dropped another buoy and headed for the outward-bound wormhole. This put us, at the other end, a total of just under 50,000 light-years from the core. Literally, the edge of the galaxy.

The system we found ourselves in was a red giant. It glowed like a malignant coal in the sky, with a visible disk even from 50 AU. Again, nothing presented itself as an abode for life, and more important, there was only the one wormhole that we'd just come through.

Dead End? Goku commented, disappointment plain in his tone. Looks like it.

I'm not sure why the PGF would have bothered with a wormhole here. This is a nothing system.

In a nothing area, Goku replied. No spiral alarm here.

The star's nearest neighbor, based on a quick scan, might be 100 light-years away. Talk about isolated.

Yeah, but again, the view, I gestured, and Goku muttered. Wow. We weren't that much closer to the

Andromeda Galaxy, given the angle, but with nothing between us and our larger neighbor, it stood out Spectacularly. Maybe that's why they built a route to here, he added.

I don't know. Next spoke to anti-spinward would be better. I don't see a reason for this system to be on the network at all.

And yet there's a wormhole with two still-active shepherding satellites. Come to think of it, how is maintenance handled with those things? Do they do their own repairs or does someone do house calls?

Maybe both. Ick and Dayscanned a couple of them early on and found maintenance bots in the form of something like Roamers, PGF-style, so they're self-repairing, at least to a certain extent, and they handle comms across the wormhole interfaces so they could easily yell for help if something went wrong that was beyond their ability, unless it was enough to take one down completely. Two stations go, redundancy.

You'd have to take out both at the same time, and even then the wormhole would bleed away negative energy slowly. If you have some system of pinging nodes periodically, you could detect the outage before the wormhole closed and send replacements. I guess their engineers will be at least as paranoid as our engineers.

Transcends species, buddy. Okay, fine. Doesn't answer the question, though.

Why terminate here? Well, why not? The spoke has to end somewhere.

We're right at the edge of the galaxy. You know, if we went upward a hundred light years or so, I bet the view would be spectacular. No, there still has to be a point.

If this was a habitable system, okay. If it had links to more systems, okay. If it, maybe it was supposed to.

Goku gave me a quizzical look, so I continued. What if the builders created this link with the intention of creating links to other places? Maybe they stopped for some reason.

Uh, okay, Goku frowned in thought. Not sure how it helps us, though. I'm playing a long shot here, but let's examine this system with a fine-toothed comb.

Make sure it's as empty as it seems. Looking for what? Don't know, but I guarantee we'll know it when we see it.

Fine, Goku rolled his eyes dramatically. But if we're wasting our time, I'm going to hold it over you forever. I expect no less.
