Will. In Virt. December 2347.

I spent very little time querying the 82 Eridani colonies. The governments of Asgard, Valhalla, and Takama had never forgotten their helplessness when Medeiros pulled that sneak attack back in 22-23, and their military budgets reflected it. This attacker, whoever they were, was only able to take out some of the more remote installations before the entire 82 Eridani navy responded like a disturbed hornet's nest.

Things were currently at a stalemate, which wasn't ideal, but at least nothing more was getting damaged. Before I'd even said proper goodbyes, I got a notification of an emergency UFS council session. I'd been expecting it, and the only reason it had taken this long was the slow human frame rate.

I still had a seat on the council, but as a member at large rather than as a representative of any particular group. The UFS didn't want to officially recognize the Bobiverse as a power block, but they privately recognized that, like it or not, we were the proverbial 800-pound gorilla in many ways. The result was best described as cordial, but strained.

I retreated to my VR and popped up the session window. About half the membership had already arrived and more were connecting as I watched. It was a measure of the urgency of the situation, that we had a quorum less than five minutes after the notice, and even more significant, that the President called us to order the moment that became true.

Fellow Safontes, he began, we are under attack by unknown forces. I would like to suspend the normal forms and proceed directly to discussion. You should have a summary in your info streams of everything that is currently known.

It will be updated in real time as more information becomes known. and with that he sat back and his speaking light went out. Request lights came on for about three quarters

of the attendees. The chair algorithm selected one, Counselor Chandra, representing Aurora's northern continent.

I want to know why we didn't have more warning, she declared. These attacks came out of nowhere.

Do we not have early warning systems? She extinguished her light to give up the floor. Other lights immediately came on.

A post appeared in the info. I was not a stream. Ships did not come in hot in any kind of attack wave.

Instead, they moved into place over a period of hours or days, acting like routine traffic. All had legitimate appearing IDs and destinations. The lack of a UFS Central Vessel Registry, discussed in previous sessions, made positive real-time confirmation impossible.

I snorted. The Navy rep had phrased that very well, but the bottom line was that council infighting and refusal to concede autonomy had been blocking integration of data systems among colonies for decades. This was, in my opinion, inevitable, but pointing that out wouldn't do any good.

It did, however, put out about half the request lights.

The session went through several more versions of complaints about the situation before the chair took control. The situation is what it is. A search for the guilty at this time will neither improve the current state of affairs nor produce strategies for dealing with it.

Can we please limit our commentary to productive questions and suggestions?

I could see a lot of reactions in individual windows, and I was able to lip-read in some cases. People apparently didn't appreciate being lectured or deprived of their chance to kvetch. I decided to toss in my two pams worth just to stir things up.

I pressed the request button and was immediately recognized by the chair.

Representatives, the Bobiverse, as always, stands ready to help wherever we can. We are not a military organization, but we've had our share of space battles and we do have extensive manufacturing capability to offer. I released the floor, sat back, and grinned.

Let the shitstorm begin. And begin it did. The Bavaverse was tolerated at best in civilized society.

Some of it was deserved, of course. In particular, the whole Starfleet thing had strongly affected meatspace through no fault of the humans. But the animosity had been there long before that.

Several reps took the floor to tell us to essentially mind our own business and stay out of it. Don't call us, we'll call you. Sure thing.

Finally, someone with some sense came on. Shamal Ouray from the Covenant Colony took the floor and said, Honored representatives, and I use the term loosely today, I think this is a case where beggars can't be choosers.

Like it or not, we are currently getting our collective asses kicked, some more than others, so don't at me with well-actualies. Our navies, while valiant and courageous, cannot perform miracles such as shooting down foes with empty cannons.

If you cannot stomach dealing with the Bobiverse even to the extent of accepting a consignment of munitions from them, perhaps you should reconsider your career choices. I chuckled.

The sarcasm was strong with this one. Of course, Shemal was definitely not a career politician. He had a well-earned reputation for impatience with the terminally self-inflated.

Anytime he took the floor, it was a fair bet someone was going to end up with scorch marks. There was a pause, then Representative Lila Jukovic from Caledonia Nova took the floor. Gentle beings, it is imperative that we work together to defeat our attackers.

In addition to accepting any help the Bobiverse would care to give, I suggest we have our respective navies liaison to shift assets as necessary to shore up weak defenses. We are in a unique situation in that we can now move from system to system in minimum time, assuming the enemy doesn't take down the wormhole system. I quickly added a note to the info stream.

Wormnet administration has closed all portals to prevent the enemy from using the system. They can be reopened at any time on two minutes' notice to shuttle assets between systems. The portal satellites are potentially vulnerable, but they would have to be knocked out on both sides

for any given portal to be rendered permanently useless.

Djukovic paused while she read the note, then continued. Then it is in our best interests to assign assets to guard and defend the portals. I call on all colonial governments to immediately instruct individual navies to coordinate with each other.

She then released to the floor and sat back. The argument raged for almost half an hour between those who didn't want to give up autonomy and those who didn't want to get wiped out.

It was no surprise at all to me that the colonies most reluctant to pitch in were those that were under the least pressure at the moment.

Stupid humans. The rest of this session would very likely be predictable. I sighed and assigned an

avatar to take notes and give me a summary, and to page me if I was asked a question.
