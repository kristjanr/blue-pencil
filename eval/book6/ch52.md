We had a couple of weeks of free time before we'd have to worry about the portal in NGC-6934. Even then, unless something happened that required our attention, it would just be a matter of instructing the cargo

drones appropriately. I hoped that the satellites wouldn't object to being tethered long-term, but I I had to admit that if there was anything the PGF could do about it, someone would have shown up by now to do maintenance or pest removal.

We decided to try circumnavigating the galaxy at the outer ring. The distances at 50,000 light years were much greater, but wormhole jumps didn't seem to care. According to physics theory, there was no limit to how far apart portals could be,

limited by travel time to put them there in the first place, of course. In any case, the ring petered out quickly and we hadn't found any other jumps to globular clusters or any other kind of external stellar system. I commented on this to Goku.

Yeah, Ikinday's blog talks about the lack of connections to non-viable systems. It really looks like the NGC-6934 portal was an exceptional situation. Either the network engineers created that connection specifically for some good reason, or they at some point disconnected all the dangerous portals except that one.

I sighed.

Someone, someday, is going to have to figure all this out. Well, how about this, Goku said. What if the PGF only created the hubs, the rings and spines?

What if all the residential connections were created by the individual species? Once they connected to the network, they wouldn't really have any incentive to go casting about on their own. Or if they did, I replied, warming to the thought, they'd do it like we have.

Connect from home to the first system, then connect that to the second system, and so on. All the exploratory connections would be in the local networks. And if they connected to an interesting but non-habitable system, they might or might not keep the connection alive.

So, should we be delving into local networks more? To find these interesting systems? Pass, Goku replied.

Not that it wouldn't be interesting, but we've got other fish to fry right now. Like, how about we go to the innermost ring and try to circumnavigate that? Sounds like a plan.

It took more than a week to do all the hops, but finally we were in the inner ring, 5,000 light-years from the core. The view was, of course, spectacular. The next step would be to try to hop around to the far side of the galaxy, or at least as close as we could manage.

This section of the PGF network was sparse, both in terms of total connections and in terms of local connections.

In fact, none of the local connections could be definitively identified as inhabited or even habitable systems.

We left a buoy at every new hub, trusting that Bill's automated system would eventually bring BobNet to us, or at worst, would carry our final entry back to the Bobiverse.

* * *

Maintenance. We really couldn't tell. At least at first.

Eventually, though, in Hub 170, we hit paydirt of a sort. We were doing the usual, cataloging the portals before deciding what to do next, when Goku uttered the two most ominous words in the English language.

That's odd. Oh, you're going to have to be a lot more specific, I growled. Curious odd or time to leave odd?

Curious. This portal here, Goku tapped the list, is not PGF. What?

I noticed we had one less entry on the spreadsheet than the number of portals we can detect. This one doesn't respond to standard queries. He paused.

I suppose this could be some species that connected up to the network with their own hardware like we did, so we might be about to meet another spacefaring race. Oh, that is just so interesting. Are we going to take a look?

Goku shook his head. I think one of our drones is going to have that honor. We had this whole bit down to a routine.

In short order, the drone was coasting through the portal connected by a maser link. Icarus and Daedalus had published the app they used to identify the location of the other end from star charts. The drone sent back pictures and we immediately ran the program.

It seems to be on Ring 2 but on Spoke 9. We dead-ended on Ring 2 on Spoke 7. This is like a detour route or something.

Cute, Goku replied. Any portals? I issued the command and in a few seconds the remote drone had delivered a result.

The PGF protocol produced no responses, but the drone has detected two microwave signatures reasonably close by, so this is not part of the PGF network.

Maybe a local civilization? Goku made a face. I'm not happy with this, Kal.

We're dropping buoys this far out, and we don't know if this network has different rules or maintenance cycles than the PGF network. If our buoys get taken down before anyone reads them, we'll have just vanished.

I thought briefly of chiding Goku, but realized something had changed in our relationship. We weren't at each other's throats nearly as much. In fact, we'd actually, on one or two occasions, been nice to each other.

Was it the realization of shared danger? No, we'd shared danger before.

The onset of maturity? Unlikely for any Bob, and Bob 1 would be the first to admit it.

I decided that it had to be watching other pairs of Bobs, like Ick and Day or Neil and Herschel getting along. And let's face it, the Calvin and Hobbes debacle was now literally century-level old news. We can fly back to the last BobNet node and request a priority update on our chain of hubs.

I bet Bill would do it without argument. Goku nodded. I'd feel better if we did.

I'd feel even better if we just went back and grabbed a bunch of communication relays to distribute ourselves. That'll slow us down, though. We'll either have to fly all the way back to Epsilon Eridani, that's 13 hops if you're keeping track, or ask Bill to send them to us.

Either could take weeks.

Don't care, Goku replied. I think this is where I dig in my heels, Kal. I sighed.

Fine, we'll do it your way. I didn't want to admit it, but I was relieved that Goku had forced the issue We started hopping back to Epsilon Eridani and found, to our happy surprise, that the automated comms seeders had almost caught up with us and had reached Ring 1

As soon as we had a connection, we sent a ping to Bill And got a rejection, along with the note A little busy, guys, unless this is urgent, send an email and I'll get to it when I can

Well, I'm offended, I said, trying my best to look hard done by At least he didn't try to draft us, send the email I rolled my eyes and composed a request for priority update of comms relays to 1-7-0.

At the same time, I asked if we could confiscate a half dozen relays for manual distribution. We'd made two more hops before Bill replied, which was fairly concerning. How busy was he anyway?

Was the war going that badly?

Sure, no prob. I've updated the distribution system's orders and also set aside six relay packages for you. You'll have to pick them up in Epsilon Eridani unless you want to arrange a drop-off location.

I sent Bill an acknowledgment, then gave Goku a smirk. It feels like we're wearing a groove into the space lanes.

Several days later, we found ourselves back in 1-7-0. The Cedars had beaten us there, having continued to build the network while we were on our pilgrimage, and we took a moment to feel the relief at having full BobNet communications capability.

Ready to go exploring? Goku said. Hold on to your buttcheeks.

Let me deploy this on the portal. Hopefully it doesn't have a more enthusiastic cleanup crew. I frowned at the thought.

Where would it come from? Could there be an entire civilization on this side that we haven't seen yet? I would think they'd have left long before the PGF.

They would have seen Nemesis first.

Whatever. Deployment's done. Let's boogie.

We aimed our vessels and transited the portal before we had time for second thoughts. We needn't have worried. It was a total non-event.

We found ourselves in a system with an F-type star, a relative rarity anywhere, and even more so this close to the core. Should we look for planets? Goku asked.

Why? Think someone's gonna want to send a colony here? I gave Goku a side-eye.

Let's just check out the two portals, okay? Goku shrugged and made an after-you gesture. I picked one of the two portals at random and started for it.

The transition was uneventful and no space vampires waited on the other side. There was a sun, the typical red dwarf that makes up 80% of all stars. But SUDAR started reporting detections.

Many, many detections. Yipe, should we run? I said, staring at the holotank.

The telemetry showed that the detections were all metallic, with the kind of readings that suggested manufactured items.

Spaceships? Missiles? Drones?

Mines? Mechanical space whales? That's a lot of...

um, Goku hesitated. But no response to our presence. No scans.

No one has turned in our direction to give chase.

Hold on. He worked his telescope for a moment. Infrared doesn't register anything.

Whatever those are, they're at background temperature. Some kind of scrapyard? maybe?

So no immediate danger. Nevertheless, Goke, I feel the need to report this and see if anyone in the group wants to join us.

Your paranoia is noted and approved. Let's do that. Without another word,

we turn tail and beat a somewhat dignified retreat.
