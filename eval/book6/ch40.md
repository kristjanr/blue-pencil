I had just finished a quick discussion with Teresa about the battle. She was now up to her elbows in diplomatic discussions with the Pav about continued military support and declined to join me for the meeting that Bill had called. I happily left her to it and popped over to Bill's at the appointed time.

I was the last one to arrive. Bill, Will, Howard, and Garfield were all sitting around nursing drinks. It was immediately obvious who had the news.

Both Bill and Garfield were all but vibrating. I sank into a cushioned seat and said, Good or bad, Bill chuckled.

Good, mostly, although it comes with a hefty dose of for fuck's sake, Garfield laughed. Yep, it's never simple. Okay, let's start with the basics, Will said.

Hit us. Bill struck a hero pose, paused dramatically, then said, FTL, we has it. Dead silence for several mils, then Howard exclaimed, you are shitting me.

I shit you not, Bill replied. We have actually exceeded the speed of light using what I would honestly characterize as a warp drive, a variation on an Alcubierre drive based on what I was able to learn from examining a derelict ship that Calvin and Goku towed back here from the PGF network. So we no longer need wormholes?

Howard asked. He actually looked a little freaked out. It seemed General Bull Moose was concerned for his bottom line.

Well, I wouldn't go that far. Bill paused. First, we've only gotten it up to 1.5C.

We're not even close to warp 2 yet. I think we're actually about warp 1.14. The problem is that the drive is kind of a power hog.

There's no theoretical reason to expect any limitations, though. It'll be like airplanes. The Wright brothers' first flight achieved a blazing 34 miles an hour airspeed.

By 1947, we'd broken the sound barrier. So, just engineering, really, I said. That doesn't sound like a big enough problem to justify your negativity, Bill.

What's the kicker? Bill gave me a small smile. The kicker is a kind of reverse time dilation, although it really isn't that.

Basically, no matter how fast you go, internal time passes at one year per light year traveled.

What? Several people exclaimed at once. Bill continued.

If you travel four light years, four years will pass for the occupants of the ship. Doesn't matter if you travel the distance at sea or ten times sea or one hundred times sea, passengers experience four years. Well, that's...

Howard glared at Bill. That's pointless. Well, no, it's not a wormhole, so you can carry a wormhole endpoint with you.

In fact, the Dutchman, the derelict that Calvin and Goku found, was doing exactly that, laying out wormhole connections. How fast was it traveling between stops? Will asked.

Can't really tell. There was too much damage, although the power delivery systems were immense. But look, if you can do 10C and bring a wormhole endpoint with you, you'll still get a new portal in one-tenth the time of a SURGE-equipped delivery system.

Faster, really, since the warp drive doesn't have acceleration and deceleration phases. It just snaps to full speed and back to zero. But because of the time thing, you'd have to use an automated ship system, or a replicant, or humans in stasis pods.

Will leaned forward in his chair, becoming enthusiastic. And if it's a bob, you could just frame Jack down to make the time pass faster. Hell, most of us do that anyway on long flights, even with time dilation.

There was a long silence, then Garfield said, If we could get the light speed multiple up high enough, we could get out to Nemesis before it slams into the galaxy.

And do what? Will demanded. Whack it with a stick?

It's 200 million solar masses. I don't know, Will.

But assuming we can do something, at least now we can get there to try, Garfield replied a little testily.

Wormhole? I suggested. Jump it to the other side of the galaxy?

Bill looked up briefly before refocusing on me. The event horizon on Nemesis would be about 590 million kilometers in diameter. We have to strain to get a wormhole 10 kilometers wide.

Some work required there, I think. Plus, Garfield added, I'd like to test out what pushing a black hole through a wormhole does first. That strikes me as a significant risk.

We all laughed, somewhat ruefully. No one had forgotten the Thoth incident.

Pusher plates? Howard said. Again, Bill looked up, as he did an internal calculation.

I had to build 800 pusher plates for a world the size of Mercury during the Others' War. Ignoring for the moment that the tech has improved since then, and we probably don't need the same level of acceleration, it would require... 26.4 quintillion pusher plates.

He blinked and looked around the room. That seems like a lot. That got Snickers.

Will added, we'd have to convert half the galaxy into pusher plates. Well, no, Bill corrected. In terms of straight mass, a pusher plate is about 20,000 kilograms, so we'd only need the mass of two mercury-sized planets in total.

Seriously? Law of large numbers, guys. Things aren't always intuitive.

Of course, Bill continued. That's just in terms of mass. Like I said, pusher plates aren't made of just iron and nickel and silicate, so

We'd probably have to mine a lot more planets and asteroids to get enough copper and gold and the various platinum group elements and rare Earths, but it's not that much in terms of raw numbers.

We, that is, the Bobs and humans, have probably mined that much or more since Bob 1 left Earth.

Jeebus, Howard said. Can we do that? Can we not?

We'd be performing galactic surgery, I said. Where does that put us on the Kardashev scale? Bill shook his head slowly.

Not that high, really. Guys, this is just a matter of engineering. It's not even any new technology except the warp drive to get us there in time.

We just need to build a very, very large hammer. We all looked at each other in silence. I couldn't help thinking that, regardless of the quality or size of the hammer, this was going to put to shame everything we'd ever done.
