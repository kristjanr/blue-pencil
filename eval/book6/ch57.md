I asked. He gave me a perplexed look. Printer.

You upgraded a month ago to the latest version. Did you not read the new features list? Uh, honestly, Gar, I just let Guppy handle tech upgrades.

I didn't even realize anything had changed. I gestured. So, the apple?

Organics can now be... well, it's basically Star Trek replicators, Bill. Can I get tea?

Earl Grey. Hot. Garfield laughed.

First thing I tried, and yes, but you have to use a Shakespearean voice.

I've been told many times that our Picard impression sucks. I replied, grinning. I'd probably end up with

instant coffee, no water. Garfield snickered but didn't respond. Instead, he tossed the apple core over the

Organics. It'll probably be gone in an hour. You have bunnies, right?

Or in about 10 years, I'll have an apple tree there. So what are you working on, Bill? What makes you think I'm...

Never mind, dumb question. I'm still trying to figure out how to get more multiples out of the warp drive. Still not happy with 42X?

It's not that I'm unhappy. It just seems mediocre. Was the PGF laying out the wormhole network at 42X?

Why not, Bill? It would be fast enough if it was done by automated systems, which we're pretty sure the Dutchman was. Yeah, but it wasn't doing 42x.

I've managed to rough together a 3D graph of power input versus field size versus mass, and if I've got it right, the Dutchman would have been doing about 1.4x. That's almost not worth it. A regular SURGE drive-equipped ship could do the same job with a lot less power, and time dilation would guarantee reduced maintenance requirements.

The only explanation is that their power curve is different from ours. You have a point. Are we missing something?

You mean, was the Dutchman able to travel much faster than we think? I think so, Gar.

On the one hand, the predicted multiplier for the Dutchman is pointless, if true, but on the other hand, if the PGF has warp drive that's faster than a certain multiple, they'd have already reached the Magellanic Clouds. They left 2,000 years ago, and the large Magellanic Cloud is around 170,000 light years, depending on where you start from, so if they have more than 85x, they'd already be there. Are we sure they aren't?

We assume they brought wormholes. I mean, why wouldn't they? So you'd think they'd have come back through.

if only to turn off the lights. Lots of assumptions, Bill. Sure, but reasonable ones.

I mean, think about what they have to carry. Auto factories of sufficient size to boot up the PGF society. Drones and roamers or their equivalent.

The matrices or their equivalent to hold the patterns of I don't know how many quadrillions of people and thousands of planets can't be small. I grinned at him. Lots of assumptions, Gar.

But reasonable ones, he said, grinning back. So you're saying we still have the minimal size option. I stopped and thought about it.

Yeah, but then what's the difference? Do it now, do it later. Seems to me that having people live with the idea for 2,000 years might be a bit stressful.
