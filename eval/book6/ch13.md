After leaving the PGF membership issue with Will to Handle, we headed back into the network. Elmer had volunteered to head outward one ring and go clockwise to see how far it went, so we set our sights on

doing the same, with the first ring outward from Galactic Center. Other bobs were mapping hubs, checking out other spokes, and so forth.

We'd picked tasks in the most general terms, but everyone agreed that there were just too many unknowns to be able to really nail down any kind of project plan. The buoys set

up with a store and forward style of blogging we're going to have to do until we could get on the PGF communication network. This ring was at about 14,000 light years from center.

At this distance, the central bulge of the galaxy totally dominated the view. I resolved to find a planet with an atmosphere and

check out the night sky in a mani. It would surely be unforgettable. But meanwhile, we had some exploring

to do. We'd already been through the spoke hub that connected to this ring when we visited the center of the galaxy, but it had been very much a drive-by.

We hadn't even stopped to catalog all the wormhole portals. Currently, we were on Ring 1 Spoke 4 Hub 0. It was a heavily populated hub.

In terms of wormholes, almost as many as Ring 4 Spoke 4, which was now called the Capital Hub. We took the time to read the identification of every portal. We'd learned a few things from the archivist about the contents of the ID packets, but it was still mostly textual information that wouldn't mean anything to us.

I guess if you were a PGF member in good standing, intending to visit the Bargal species, you'd already know things like what they breathed and whether they might eat you. Decision time, I said today. Explore or go around.

Honestly, icky and I can't believe I would ever say this, but looking at yet another habitable but empty planet is just... kind of depressing.

Let's go look at stars. I grinned and gave him a thumbs up and we turned our ships to head for the connector portal.
