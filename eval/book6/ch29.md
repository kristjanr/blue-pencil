November 2347. I was sorting through the intelligence reports from the various human agencies when I got a text from Calvin saying that they were back in human space and would soon be arriving with the derelict. This was, of course, exciting, but I forced myself to finish my current task.

I spared a moment to acknowledge the text, then dug back in. There were at least three disappearances of non-Bob ships in the last two years that had been marked unexplained. All single ships, not near population centers.

In two cases, debris had been found near the last estimated location. So, assuming the same unknown perpetrator was responsible, they weren't targeting just Bobs. I found that to be more of a relief than I should have.

After all, a bio getting blown up was a lot more permanent. Unfortunately, it didn't give us much more information. It could still be random violence or targeted intelligence gathering.

The only other thing we knew was that it was centered around human space. There'd been no reports on the PGF side, according to the buoy updates in Malacca. I swore out of my breath, closed the file, and sent an update to the Ancients, marked Info Only.

The drones slowly pulled the Derelict into a station-keeping position near Ultima Thule. I'd taken over control from Calvin and Goku once the Derelict had reached Epsilon Eridani, as they wanted to get back to their explorations. I understood perfectly.

The acceleration that the drones could apply was abysmal, barely a tenth G. The only reason it hadn't taken longer was that the vector was applied continuously.

Even a tenth G could get you places eventually. Calvin and Goku had carefully calculated the derelict's course to minimize decelerations and changes in direction as it transitioned through the portals.

No point in spending time piling on speed only to have to spend an equal time bleeding it off. All in all, they'd done pretty good.

I was in my manny, maintaining a physical presence more out of habit than anything these days. I found it mildly ironic

on the few occasions when I gave it any thought that with all the power and near-infinite flexibility of the Bobiverse in VR, I preferred real. Setting aside the momentary distraction, I instructed my minions

to get to work. Immediately, two dozen drones filled with roamers and high-definition SUDAR scanners poured out of one of the space station's cargo bays and raced toward the Hulk. I wanted this thing analyzed down to the molecule.

Most of it would, no doubt, be unimpressive, but it would at least give me clues as to the construction standards of the makers that might help me to extrapolate the missing and destroyed sections, especially that mystery drive thing. Hey, Bill, Garfield said as he popped in, appearing to me in AR. I got your invitation.

I don't remember sending one, I replied, frowning. Yeah, funny that. I'm mortally offended.

He gave me an arch look, his fists on his hips. I laughed and pointed at my holotank display. Go get your manny.

They'll be at the Hulk in five minutes. Garfield arrived with moments to spare, grabbing an office chair and wheeling it around. Alien tech, always fun, he observed.

Also scary sometimes. Hmm, I responded noncommittally. Have you noticed that when we find alien hardware, it's always bad news?

Well, the others, yeah, he replied. But the Quinlan's, right, okay, home planet destroyed, locked in a megastructure. Bad example. And the PGF, I supplied. Fleeing the galaxy and leaving everything behind. Okay, okay, everyone's having a bad millennium.

Garfield was silent for a moment as the first drone arrived and spewed out roamers. But if this thing is PGF, I don't think it really counts.

23,000 years, Gar, and a written language completely different from PGF interlock. I'm seriously thinking of asking someone to chase down Ick and Dae and have them ask the archivist. Kal and Goku will update the shared blog on each buoy as they travel, but if they don't intersect Ick and Dae's route, it won't get picked up.

Still no network connectivity, I guess. No. I'm still trying to decide if there's anything we can do to get around the problem.

Garfield started to reply, then pointed and exclaimed, Oh, hey, roamers are in. I popped up a couple more windows on the active wall surface and we watched, entranced, as the roamers scuttled through the Hulk in all directions.

It was absolutely an alien design. Not in the same weird organic way as the engineer ships from the Aliens franchise, but more in the why-would-they-put-that-there style. The structural bracing seemed to be overkill for the size of the vessel, for instance, and although it had corridors and such for physical access, it was obviously intended to be run autonomously.

I estimated that with the extra space requirements for crew living quarters, it would have been almost twice the size. The biggest volumes were taken up by the cargo hold, chock full of pairs of wormhole kernels, and what had to be a massive negative energy generator, ten times the size of their version of the Casimir generator.

I wonder why they didn't use antimatter, Gar mused. I don't think regular energy was a bottleneck for this unit, and they'd have to restock every once in a while. A Casimir generator will outlast the ship.

Certainly did in this case. Again, nyak-nyak. I paused as a gaggle of roamers converged on what I thought of as the real prize,

The thing that looked a lot like it could be an Alcubierre drive. At least, it seemed to be the target of the negative energy generator, so if it wasn't a drive, it was something even weirder.

Scans coming in, I said, and popped up a couple of new windows on top of the existing ones. We watched as a 3D CAD image gradually coalesced. I flicked a finger and it moved from the physical wall screen to an AR hologram floating in midair between us.

It's getting harder and harder to keep track of when we're in real and when we're in vert, Gar observed in a distracted tone. The first time you walk off a cliff and don't levitate, you'll figure it out. I replied with a smile.

I reached out and tweaked the hologram with a finger and it began to rotate slowly. Will you be able to make anything of that? Gar said.

One of the biggest unknowns of the 21st century gray box Alcubierre designs was always the negative energy delivery system. We have that now. The other was how it would be applied to space-time to create the actual warp bubble, but with SURGE drives we pretty much have a lock on that part.

I paused. The biggest remaining unknown, really, is that there is no theoretical framework to be able to predict exactly how it should operate. Once you step out of Einsteinian space, all bets are off.

That's a lot of words for not answering my question, Gar said. Fine, yes, I think I can reproduce whatever they had here. I just don't know what it'll do.

Wonderful, as usual, we're winging it. Life in the Bobiverse, Gar, are we having fun yet?
