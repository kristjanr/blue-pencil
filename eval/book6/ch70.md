I popped into Bill's VR to find him sitting in a lawn chair roasting a marshmallow over a fire.

What the? I looked around. No one else had arrived yet.

We haven't done this since... Well, other than the one Charles hosted after the recolonization vote. It was in 2189, 159 years ago.

It was Howard's idea last time. Bill gave me a shrug. I just got a sudden burst of nostalgia.

It sometimes feels like we're drifting further and further from what made us human in the first place. What made us human? Or what made us young?

I get what you're complaining about, Bill. I felt the boredom occasionally, too, but I wonder, if we were fully human but 300 years old, would we feel differently? Or is this just what happens when you live that long?

Bill was just opening his mouth to reply when Howard and Will popped in at virtually the same time. Cool, Howard said and materialized a stick and marshmallow without missing a beat. Will smiled and held his hands out to the fire.

In short order, Annick and Garfield showed up, then Bridget and Bobby, and we had our quorum. I glanced at Bridget, hopefully without being too obvious. She usually showed up with Howard.

Instead, she and Bobby were whispering and laughing. It looked like a friendship was developing. Annick was confused by the campfire.

You do not need to warm yourselves in VR. What is the purpose? I thought of all the ways that could be answered, but the simplest response was the best.

A campfire has cultural significance over and above the practical uses. As such, we find it calming and reassuring.

Understood. Do you need calming at this time? I thought that was the purpose of beer.

I chuckled. Sometimes that makes humans belligerent. Quinlans, too, if I remember correctly.

Anyway, coffee does the trick for us. ANEC shook his head. Depressants stimulate and stimulants sedate.

Humans are annoying.

Amid the chuckles, Howard changed the subject. So, Bill, you called the meeting. I'm guessing it's about the black hole test?

Among other things, we still need to do the test, and I'm hoping with the war over, we can commit some auto-factory time to pusher plates Is it really that big of a gamble, though? Howard asked

Moving the black hole with pusher plates, I mean, it's just a question of scale, isn't it? Not quite You know the phrase, quantity has a quality all its own?

Uh-huh, Howard said, sensing a punchline Well, a 200 million solar mass black hole has a quality all its own as well Frame dragging, event horizon, time dilation.

So many things that can affect the outcome. So many things that can go wrong. We might need more plates than we thought.

Or we might need to beef them up. Or both. But that's just engineering, right?

Bill bobbed his head back and forth. Probably it's not new physics anyway, but it's one more headache, and if any part of the extrapolations goes non-linear, we could still be hooped. Garfield piped up.

But we can get started with a warp drive wormhole pipeline, right? We have a reasonable expectation in principle that we can redirect Nemesis, We have a starting point on the other side of the galaxy to build the pipeline from, thanks to Calvin and Goku.

We have enough good star systems identified that we can use for construction. Is there anything else that can get in our way? Probably, said three people at once.

Fair enough, Bill said. So let's get started now that we've shoveled all the other crap out of the way.

Jeez, hasn't this been a fun year, Garfield muttered, sarcasm dripping from every syllable.

I gave him an amused look. Honestly, Garfield, I haven't really noticed a difference. It just feels like every year has some variation on disaster, catastrophe, emergency, or apocalypse.

Sometimes I seriously regret not having aimed my ship for intergalactic space the first time I left Epsilon Eridani. Will chuckled. Sure, but think of all those disasters, catastrophes, emergencies, and apocalypses you'd have missed out on.

Um, I replied, unconvinced. Bill looked around the room.

So we need to talk about future plans, assuming some other disaster doesn't loom unexpectedly. Ridley. Like, Will said.

What, disasters or plans? Howard interjected. Dealer's choice, I said, grinning.

Okay, Howard replied. Will, quick question for you. I just read some of your Earth recolonization blog and there was something about printing trees.

Is that right? Will nodded. Yes.

And I had pretty much the same reaction when Charles demonstrated the technology. It's energy intensive, of course, but it takes less than 30 seconds to produce a full adult tree. Howard glanced over at Bridget, who was paying very close attention, almost glaring at Will with the intensity of her gaze.

Then he asked, How big? Potentially? I don't think there's a theoretical limit.

It's just a question of how big a replicator you want to build. We're calling them replicators now? I interjected.

Will shrugged. Well, yeah, printer just doesn't seem appropriate. one more.

I'll give only one more. Sorry. I will only have to be in this captwhomper.

We're going to be 50,000 light-years away. If history is any judge, the reaction probably won't be subtle.

He paused before continuing. We should start building the pipeline right away.

Gar's right about that. It's the critical path item in this project plan, and we have to manufacture several bajillion pusher plates.

Have I missed anything? No one spoke, but several people shook their heads in the negative.

Not for the first time, I admitted. It was going to be an interesting millennium.
