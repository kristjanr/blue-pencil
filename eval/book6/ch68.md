April 2348. The palace was massive, ornate, and somewhat Byzantine.

We'd rushed it a bit, but had managed to reconstruct a fairly faithful copy of the original Paso Imperial, the Empire of Brazil's palace. Gathered in the main ballroom were myself, Bill, Bobby, Garfield, a half-dozen hastily sworn-in Brazilian dignitaries, and Manuel Constantino, the newly-minted president of Brazil.

Manuel had, despite his complaints, been convinced, or maybe bullied was the better word, into wearing ceremonial 19th-century royal garb. He'd commented at one point that he looked and felt like a Christmas tree ornament.

Now came the moment of truth. Manuel sat down at a desk and flipped open his tablet. He opened an app that would transmit on the public's gut channel to all colonies simultaneously

Since we still didn't know where the chief Medeiros was, we'd have to let the Medeiri work that out The dignitaries positioned themselves in the background and the rest of us moved off camera Manuel sat forward, pressed the transmit button, and began to speak

Attention, Major Ernesto Medeiros This is Manuel Constantino, interim president of the reconstituted state of Brazil I am a direct descendant of Dona Maria, de Gloria, Joana Carlotta, Leopoldina, de Cruz, Francisca, Xavier, de Paola, Isidora, Micaela, Gabriela, Rafaela, Gonzaga, de Habsburgo, Lorena e Braganza, daughter of Emperor Don Pedro I, and Empress Dona Maria Leopoldina.

As such, I am the rightful heir to the throne of the Empire of Brazil, and given that, you are by your oath and rank answerable to me. You will respond immediately.

Manuel pushed the tablet away, then turned to us and grinned sheepishly. That was a mouthful, and I hope I don't have to repeat it. We could have recorded it, I replied.

But she wanted to be the brinksman. I gave him a thumbs up. Nicely done, Manuel.

Now we wait to see if he buys it.

Manuel looked around at the assembled individuals. Unlike the various Bobs who were dressed casually, the putative Brazilian officials looked almost as garish and uncomfortable as Manuel. But all were willing to play the part.

And we didn't know if Medeiros would accept a video feed, so it might be set dressing for nothing. But as much as possible, we wanted a connection with the original empire. If Bob's analysis of Medeiros was correct, these things would sway him even more than legalities

There was a click, then This is Major Ernesto Medeiros, I note from the communications envelope that your message originates on Earth President Constantino, you know the phrase trust, but verify

I accept the possibility that you are who you claim to be, but without confirmation it is just words I await suggestions to improve the situation Manuel grinned and I did a fist pump

mutters of approval floated around the room from other attendees. We'd hoped for exactly this and had a script ready. Manuel picked up the mic and switched it on.

Of course, Major. I understand completely. Here is my suggestion.

Send a single ship to Earth. The UFS will make a wormhole portal available so that this can be accomplished in days rather than years. We assume that you have remote observation drones and possibly even manis.

Send one down to the palace. We're at the site of the original Paso Imperial. There we will discuss such proofs as may be acceptable

to establish my authority. Manuel paused and immediately Medeiros responded. I appreciate the

offer of a wormhole traversal, Mr. President, but as it happens I have a presence in the soul system. I will send one of my drones to your location with all haste.

Expect it in 1.5 days, Medeiros out.

That wasn't unexpected, I said to the room once Manuel had switched off. The fact that Medeiros even had ships lurking around the outer reaches of Epsilon Eridani would have clued us in that he covered all

the bases. So why no attack on Earth? Manuel asked.

Same as with Epsilon Eridani, I think. Bill chimed in. He could put ships out in the Oort without anyone noticing, but not in the inner system.

Neither Sol nor Epsilon Eridani are high-traffic locations, and any unidentified ships would be noticed right away. And there's no strategic value to either system. I looked around at the people in the ballroom.

Well, looks like we'd better do some rehearsals for a formal reception. The drone had an uncanny resemblance to those developed by the Bobs. Yet another indication that Medeiros had raided the public UFS archives over the years for technology.

I found this oddly comforting, since it meant that he was an end-user rather than a creator. Items like the warp drive and wormholes would remain beyond his reach as long as the engineering details weren't publicly posted. It was big, though, almost three feet in diameter.

That was unnecessary considering how much we'd managed to miniaturize such things and made me suspicious that it might have explosives on board. or Romers or worse. Some of the people in the room were Manny's, some Huey's, but there were still plenty of base model humans.

It had been decided that the actual Brazilian officials should show up in person, lest Medeiros see it as some kind of trick.

But that meant that Manuel and the others were at physical risk. Manuel was wearing AR-enabled contacts and a dermal patch transceiver. We'd insisted on that, and he hadn't particularly balked.

I sent a text to him about my suspicions and thought I detected a slight nod. Is there a reason for the size of your remote, Major? Manuel asked.

It seems excessive. An electronic voice emanated from the drone in a somewhat muffled version of Medeiros' voice.

It doubles as a small transport drone, Mr. President. I have had no need for the miniaturized versions to which you refer.

Huh. Either that was honest or Medeiros was a quick thinker. Manuel nodded, then produced a tablet.

I have a file here that establishes my bona fides and those of the other officials gathered here.

It will also serve as an introduction and description of our authorities and duties. Are you ready to receive? I am.

Manuel tapped the tablet. There was a short pause. Then Medeiros said,

Received. I will have to take time to review this in detail, but at first glance it does appear to bear out your claims.

Very well, Major. Let us begin. You have stated that your goal is the establishment of a Brazilian state, in broadest terms.

Is this a reasonable statement? Yes, in broadest terms, there are detailed requirements. Of course.

So, let me ask, what are the metrics that you would require in order to be convinced of our legitimate claim to the legacy of your former government? Hmm. A pause.

The ideal metric, of course, would be continuity of government, but I don't believe you can deliver that. No, Major. And if you adhere inflexibly to that requirement, then neither can you deliver on your intended goal.

There is nothing that can be interpreted as such. Therefore, any legitimate claimant must be a re-establishment rather than a continuation. Do you agree?

Hmm. Yes, provisionally. And I believe you mentioned persons of Brazilian descent?

Yes, of course. A Brazilian state without actual Brazilian citizens would be a travesty. We have provided records that show the officials in this room fulfill that requirement.

The same records can establish similar bloodlines for tens of thousands of persons. If you accept the documentation as authoritative, then we have fulfilled that requirement.

If you do not accept, then again you have no possible way to achieve your goals, even in principle.

Manuel was playing it perfectly.

We discussed this at length. Based on ANEC's analysis, Medeiros' imperatives would feel to him like a strong desire. He would want to agree with Manuel if it would get him closer to his goal.

He would very much resist any logic or conclusion that would stymie that goal. It would create a strong feeling of confirmation bias.

That is true, sir, Medeiros replied. Now, having a state of Brazil situated in the traditional territory of Brazil, governed by and populated by descendants of Brazilian citizens, is a good start. Is it not?

It is, sir. If your original government had decided to change its policies or its name or even its form of government by peaceful means, you would have continued to be loyal, correct? Yes, sir, because, as an officer of the military, it is not only not your job to dictate or decide policy, but it is in fact your job to studiously avoid doing so.

Otherwise, you end up with juntas, and none of us want those. I agree, sir. So I submit for your consideration that this government, this state, is completely compatible with your stated goals, as well as your oath as an officer of the military.

Yes, Mr. President. And that was it.

The acknowledgement.

Bill, Garfield, and I gave each other simultaneous thumbs-ups. Bobby grinned and struck a hero pose. Do you need time to review this, Major?

I don't believe so, sir. I can see no flaws in the chain of logic. Excellent.

If I may, in anticipation of this outcome, I have arranged a small ceremony to welcome you into the arms of the reconstituted state of Brazil and to install you as its commander-in-chief. Honest to God, if a remote drone could draw itself up to rigid attention, that was exactly what it would look like. I could swear the drone became a more perfect sphere.

Of course, Mr. President, said Medeiros with obvious pride. The ceremony was short, loaded with symbolism, but carefully not too pompous.

We didn't want to give Medeiros any reason to be skeptical or to feel that he was being played. At the end, Manuel awarded Medeiros a shiny new rank and a flowery title, which he had insisted was pretty much par for the course for their traditional culture. Now, General Medeiros, on to business.

You have been engaged in a war with the colonies of the UFS with the goal of creating a Brazilian state. That goal is achieved. In addition, the reconstituted state of Brazil is officially at peace with those other colonies.

It is therefore appropriate that you cease hostilities forthwith. Of course, Mr. President.

There was no bluster, or defiance, at all, in Medeiros' voice. He had bought in completely, or he was the greatest actor since Jonathan Frakes. The President, for that's really what he was, and the new Commander-in-Chief continued on, discussing ways and means, bureaucracy, standing armies, and so forth.

I glanced at the other Bobs in the room and a wordless message was exchanged. As one, we sent our manis to rack themselves and popped into Bill's pub. Woo, friggin' hoo!

Ow, watch it, buster! She exclaimed in mock outrage. Screw you, we're siblings, I get to be mean to you, Garfield replied with a grin.

The nerd kind, of course. Jeeves began handing out beverages, and we parked ourselves at a table that magically expanded to accommodate all present. Say, does this pup serve food?

Wonderful. Jeeves? Bacon, cheeseburger, and fries, all the trimmings, please, and a chocolate shake.

Ah, I said, staring at the food, feeling myself unexpectedly salivating. Suddenly the idea of food was all-consuming. Hey, Bobby snapped at me.
