We jumped five times, cataloging the systems and dropping a buoy at each one. This ring appeared to be busier than Ring 4 based on the number of portals at each hub.

Of course, closer to the core, stars were packed more densely. The view was consistently fantastic. Better than the vista we'd experienced at the center of the galaxy, in fact.

There, the stars were so densely and uniformly packed that there was little or no structure to be seen, just an almost solid wall of light. It took four days to get to the last hub. This was over halfway around, which meant we had a full-on view of Nemesis, or at least the halo of stars being drawn with it directly opposite the galactic core.

Although the plane of the Milky Way partially blocked things, it was still spectacular and frightening, if you knew what it was.

I wondered idly if there were any pre-industrial races on this side that looked up at night and wondered what that was. For that matter, the core was almost certainly visible in the other half of the year, so I could see this theoretical species developing a mythology that involved two opposing deities, or maybe a god and a devil. A scholar could spend their entire life studying such a culture.
