Found something, Goku said. I quickly turned my attention to his scanning output. Sure enough, there was a faint signal of something artificial, but small and with little or no energy signature.

It was also more than a light hour away at the limit of our SUDDAR capability. When we got there, we found a couple of Shepard satellites. They looked similar but not identical to standard BGF design, but they were connected butt to butt.

It didn't seem to be an accident, either. Based on the satellite design, that looked like a deliberate configuration. What do you make of that?

I asked. Goku stared at the image, turning his head this way and that. Seems to me if you were going to grow a wormhole from scratch you'd start there with the kernel between them then move them apart as the wormhole mouth grows.

I smiled at him. Someone was paying attention in class. Someone read Bill's blogs.

It's cool stuff. I thought for a moment. But this means some other someone left a wormhole kernel here intending to open it when they reached… what?

When they reach the next place they want to open up. We're seeing the network under construction here. Should we move back?

Is there any possibility of danger? Goku shook his head. Seems to me the engineers would have allowed for this as well.

There'd be a broadcast warning and probably the thing would be grown slowly. And this is based on... Because that's how we'd have done it, Kal.

True, I thought for a moment. We have to talk to Bill. Agreed.

We dropped a beacon near the artifact and headed for the portal. We only needed to get onto the Bobiverse side of the network, then we'd be on BobNet.

Still, it was several hops in the PGF network with flight time between portals, so it was a couple of days before we were back. I messaged Bill and explained the situation,

and he invited us over to the Moot Pub. When we popped in, I looked around. Good old-fashioned pub furniture.

An oversized bar with brass railings and Jeeves behind the bar completed the picture. Bill waved to us from a circular table with raised bar chairs. I couldn't help but laugh at the incongruity.

Neither could Goku. Bill grinned at us as we walked over. This is still fairly new to you guys.

Bill, we left Epsilon Eridani before SCUT, before shareable VRs, before baseball games, before Bridget, Goku interjected. Or non-Bob replicants in general, I continued, before Goku went down that path. He'd made a couple of comments that left me concerned about his attitude toward Bridget and Howard, and I really didn't want to go there.

Bill hadn't picked up on the exchange, fortunately.

I guess you've had a lot of catching up to do, and blog reading. No kidding. Of course, it took us a while to get to the nearest WormNet portal once we connected up to BobNet, so we had lots of time to absorb things.

I pulled a chair around and sat, and Jeeves brought beers. Bill gestured to them. Try those.

If you don't like them, order something else. It's on the house. I chuckled, then sipped.

It was good. Not quite a stout, but in the same territory where it was as much a meal as a drink. I turned to face Bill squarely and said

We need to talk about wormholes You've come to the right place I hope

What's up? I explained about the artifact we'd found in 630 Bill listened intently then was silent for several mils

So what do you want to do, ideally?

I'd like to be able to find out where the other end of that wormhole is I replied We checked

There is a microscopic wormhole mouth suspended between the satellite assemblies I'm a little surprised it hasn't evaporated or something Bill shook his head

Doesn't work that way This isn't a black hole and there's no hawking radiation Once you've expanded it even a little bit, the only way for the wormhole kernel to disappear is for the other end to be merged with it, erasing the virtual particle, or let all the negative energy bleed out, but that would take a while.

Meanwhile, you drop some charge into the kernel and you can hold it captive with no power requirements. Very stable. Interesting.

I'm used. So it's still in play, so to speak. It could potentially be activated any time.

Can we expand it from this side? Goku added. I've been playing with that idea, Bill said, trying out a lot of things.

A major concern for me is what actions might turn out to be dangerous or even catastrophic. We learned more than we wanted to about collapsing wormholes by flying another portal through them in the Thoth debacle. I already knew that it would decohere violently, but I had never tried it at scale.

It was, um, impressive. I wouldn't want to be surprised by a reaction like that when I'm doing some random thing. Bill paused.

Anyway, yeah, you can force the other side open, but it takes an order of magnitude more energy to do so, on top of the energy you have to supply for the local end. Huh.

My guess is the satellites don't have the horsepower for that, I said. Highly doubtful, Bill replied.

So you'll have to provide it. Keep in mind that if it's being transported by a ship at the other end, you could damage the ship. Got it.

I don't suppose you have something that would do the trick? Bill shook his head. Sorry, Cal.

Although I could rig something up fairly quickly using multiple satellite components. It'd be pretty bulky, though. You guys are still in version two hulls, right?

Uh-huh. I glanced at Goku, who had perked up at this turn of conversation. Thinking of upgrading us?

We do have a couple of Titan-class hulls available. lots more cargo space, and you'd need all of it, I think. Let's do it, Goku said before I could reply.

Are you guys comfortable with the Skippy's transporting theory or do you want a physical matrix move? The second one, I said without hesitation. Definitely, Goku added.
