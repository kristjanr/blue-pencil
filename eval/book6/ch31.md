I guided the Fargo slowly into a high, but not synchronous, Earth orbit. It was a moment that had a lot more significance, at least for me, than the mere act would imply. This was the beginning of the repopulation of Earth.

It was the reversal of the exodus that Homer, Charles, and I had overseen for so many decades. More than that, it was a vindication of that entire chapter of our existence because these were the descendants.

Not just of Bob Johansson, but of the people who had been successfully relocated before the human race could go extinct.

But I was probably overthinking it. The viewscreen showed the Earth in gibbous phase, gradually becoming full as we orbited.

I had to admit that I wouldn't be able to tell the difference between this view and one from the early 21st century. except maybe more polar cap. I parked the manning and popped into VR as I would have better control over communications.

A quick connection to the various department heads and we were ready to rock.

We're in a stable orbit, I announced to the group. Ready for rollout. Sam, you have your site coordinates selected?

Affirmative, Will, Captain Sam McAllister replied. We're going to set down in the original Fertile Crescent. With Charles and Company's rejuvenation of the global environment, it's about as ideal a climate as you're going to find.

I nodded. I know this has been discussed, but have you had any political pushback? From the membership or from other colony worlds?

Sam smiled briefly. Some snarling on social media, of course, but Charles has made it abundantly clear that any and all prior claims, national boundaries, or entitlements were extinguished when humanity destroyed and then abandoned Earth. Fresh start.

No assumptions. There's been no friction in group, though, if that's what you're asking. Good to hear.

All right, then. Site prep group can launch at will. I'll open the end doors.

As I said it, I started opening the huge cargo doors at one end of the Fargo. They were big enough at full dilation for the USS Nimitz. to sail through with plenty of room to spare.

Within minutes, a fleet of cargo ships had left the Fargo, heading for Earth.

Transport group, I continued. You all organized? Yes, sir, responded Harold Juarez.

I suppressed a smile, easy to do in VR. Harold was ex-military and he couldn't get it out of his system. Since I was in charge, I must be an officer and you couldn't tell him otherwise.

Well, there were worse things I could be called. Over the next 16 hours, the reverse exodus executed like a, well, like a military operation. Temporary quarters were up within four hours of landing and colonists began their assigned tasks as soon as they disembarked.

I watched from numerous drones, occasionally communicating with the groundside crew chiefs, but by and large, the operation was efficient and smooth and surprisingly upbeat. The original extraterrestrial colonies were set up with a very grim, almost stoic determination to get the job done. This was different.

This was like coming home after a long vacation. I saw at least a half-dozen instances of groups spontaneously bursting into song. Usually pub songs, but you take what you get.

It was good. It was enough. We were home.
