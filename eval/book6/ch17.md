I sat in orbit around Vulcan, idly examining the view while I waited. The planet certainly looked a lot different from orbit than when I'd first arrived in this system. The night side was awash with city lights, while occasional lights floated in the oceans and seas.

On the day side of the terminator, I could see large stretches that had been cleared of the native jungles. It seemed that even with modern technology, humans heavily impacted their environment. Fortunately, with the move toward the floating cities, such clear cuts were slowly growing back in.

The UFS Council had come to a decision in an unbelievably short time. I had to wonder if someone had managed to come up with a profit argument. Certainly that would do it, if nothing else.

The immediate problem for us, though, had turned out to be a lack of available bobs that could accompany the Vulcan vessel. Will and Bob were both in interstellar space in the Fargo. Bill and Garfield were up to their elbows in various projects.

Ick and Day and about 20 other Bobs were currently spread out through the PGF, exploring, and were out of touch. Most other Bobs were either in mid-flight or were set up as Bobiverse reps on particular human worlds. And let's be honest, most of the later generation Bobs had their own goals and agendas, which, for some reason, left me.

Bridget had flat out refused to come along insisting that she'd done the Heaven's River thing and now it was my turn. Plus, she was still heavily involved in the Jabberwocky surveillance project. The dragons had managed to not die and in fact were expanding into their new territory and the scientific community was all agog at the environmental and anthropological effects.

My Matrix had been physically located at Ultima Thule for a long time now, so Bill was able to install me into one of the Titan-class vessels. Time from the space station to the wormhole portal leading to Omicron-squared Eridani, then from the portal to Vulcan, was a couple of days, most of that interval spent on the last leg. After the Thoth incident, we'd established a policy of placing the portals way far away from the colony worlds, generally out around where the Kuiper Belt would be, usually a minimum of 30 AU.

I caught myself working through this travel itinerary and realized how generally irrelevant physical distance was in the UFS nowadays.

Between Hueys and Mannys, visiting another planet was only slightly more difficult than changing your clothes. It was really only when you were leaving the UFS that your actual location began to matter.

My grousing was interrupted when I received a hail as SUDDAR informed me of the approach of one of the Vulcan military vessels. The UFS Council had nominated a representative, one Candace Newberg, a citizen of Vulcan and a member of the Council. Vulcan was supplying a Navy ship to transport her and I would be acting as guide.

BHT-2301, this is Vulcan Navy frigate Dauntless on approach for rendezvous. Bill numbered all the ships he produced these days, the designation BHT being short for Bobiverse Heaven Titan. Dauntless, this is BHT-2301, the Wolowitz, Bobiverse Titan Class Exploration Ship.

Approach at will. Wolowitz? My opposite number replied.

Not familiar with the name. Does it have significance? Only if you're 400 years old.

And a nerd. And it's better than BHT-2301. Understood, Wolowitz.

We'll come to relative stop at 200 meters, then review itinerary before departing. Eck, eck, I said in a high-pitched voice. Another 400-year-old reference?

Yeah, it's kind of a tick. Got it. Out.

The Dauntless pulled up and slid smoothly to a stop right at the indicated distance. Nice precision, if it was a human pilot. They immediately transmitted a text file containing a summary of our intended mission.

There was nothing in it that I had issue with, so I sent back an acknowledgement.

After another exchange of electronic pleasantries, we turned our vessels and aimed for the location of the Omicron-squared Eridany system wormhole. Transit would be through Epsilon Eridany. WormNet was still very hub-centric, although some colony systems were flying wormhole endpoints out to neighboring systems

so that they would eventually have direct connections and could skip a hop. The Titan-class was a powerful ship with tremendous acceleration capability, but military vessels, especially dogfighters like frigates, valued speed and maneuverability above almost everything. I found myself having to really juice the engines to keep up with the dauntless.

I suspected the nameless pilot was doing a bit of showing off. Or maybe it was the captain. Hard to say.

The trip to Abydos took a couple of days and we eventually found ourselves in the PGF's capital system. I'd read the reports from Ick and Day, but seeing the reality was something else again. This was an incredibly artificial system, which showcased an ability to move planets that the UFS and the Bobiverse hadn't come close to attempting.

Come to think of it, it might be even more impressive than was generally thought. This system consisted of six approximately evenly sized Jovian planets in an outer orbit and six terrestrial range planets in an inner.

that this system had naturally developed planets this well-matched was pretty much zero, which meant the PGF had either built the planets from whatever used to be here, or they'd moved the planets in from other systems.

Of course, Ick and Day had flown a couple of Mercury-sized planets some 30 light-years to destroy the other's home system, but the Jovians here would be a whole different level of engineering.

I sent a request to the archivist per the protocol described by Icarus and received a response. We are here to apply for provisional membership in the PGF, I said. Are you biological?

The archivist replied. I am not. However, the ship with me has bios on board, including a representative of the UFS, who will engage in formal discussions.

Acceptable. Please hold for instructions. A few seconds later, I received a large text file.

I messaged the Dauntless. Did you guys get the instructions? Affirmative, Wolowitz, hold one.

Representative Newberg would like to participate in this discussion. There was a bit of muffled commentary, then a woman's voice came on. Good day, Wolowitz.

This is Candace Newberg, Vulcan senator and UFS representative. Hello, Senator, just call me Howard.

We've discussed the situation in general terms, but now we have the specifics from the archivist. It looks like you'll be required to transfer to a small station that the

archivist will supply. You'll be scanned, shielded from communications with the rest of us for the duration, then you'll enter into discussions.

None of this looks particularly dangerous. You've read it already?

She said. Um, I read it while the comms operator was bringing you on. Replicant, remember?

Right, right. I admit I haven't had any direct contact with replicants in general except for my great aunt. who joined a post-life arcology after her passing, and she always seemed very much the same as in life.

Generally, the arcologies don't have frame-jacking or only offer it as a paid option, so that may have been accurate. Hmm, she replied noncommittally.

So does the archivist give a time frame for doing this? Immediately? Or at your convenience?

The space station is ready, environment has been customized for your comfort, and coordinates are supplied. Then let us proceed, she said. The trip to the given coordinates took only a few hours, on the way a half-dozen PGF sentry ships took up position as escorts,

They were small, obviously totally automated, and not given to conversation, despite several attempts on my part. Soon, we reached the station.

The Dauntless matched airlocks and several minutes later disconnected and took up station keeping a short distance away. I pulled up beside them.

Now we wait, I said on the common channel.
