Calvin. Epsilon Eridani. January 23, 48.

Goku and I were physically parked in station keeping a kilometer or so off Ultima Thule. I looked around using ship's sensors and realized that the area was turning into a bit of a junkyard. I spotted the pusher plates right away, two stacks of 32 plates floating a couple of clicks at plus 90 degrees.

Then there were two separate auto factory workyards at crazy angles to the station about 10 clicks away. Then about 100 clicks out, I could see what looked like Bill's warp drive fabrication yard. The actual test cradles would be out of range of a visual scan.

I knew he maintained a 10,000 click distance for the actual test runs. Off in another direction was a close cluster of asteroids that seemed to be getting harvested for raw materials, and near that were several large ingots of various metals.

And there were at least three centers of activity that I couldn't identify. Bill was a busy guy, no question.

Still, I wondered how he could stand being in the same place for literally centuries.

Sure, he got reports and updates from other Bobs, and there were all the blogs, but that was all second-hand. I did not envy him. Even with Numbnuts as a partner, exploration was still the way to go.

It's a bit of a junkyard around here, mused numbnuts. I mean, Goku. Yeah, I was just thinking the same.

Bill keeps himself busy. And speaking of, I guess we should go say hi. We popped into Bill's VR to find him pouring over several densely populated windows.

Hey, guys, he said, smiling. I was just examining the drone's ongoing scans, and I think you guys are right. The error bars are narrowing down, but they're staying within your initial estimate.

He waved to another window. I've got six plates allocated for you, control keys attached. You can take them with you when you go.

Sure, Bill. I nodded. Hey, I saw several clusters outside that I can't identify.

What you working on? Hmm. Well, Bill held up a hand and touched thumb to index finger.

I'm trying to figure out the PGF tractor beams that were used on Icarus and Daedalus at the DMZ. That would certainly be useful. He touched thumb to middle finger.

I'm trying to increase the maximum portal size that we can safely hold. If I can manage it, I think with a 20-kilometer diameter, we could accommodate your black hole safely with room to spare. He touched thumb to ring finger.

And I'm designing some warp drive-based wormhole delivery systems, subject to being able to work the kinks out of the warp drive.

Safely, Goku exclaimed. You hope safely. Well, we'll do all the testing in NGC-6934, so if things go bad, we'll know in about 50,000 years.

I laughed. And if the boom is big enough, we'll have advertised our presence to half the universe. We were soon on our way, yet again, back to NGC-6934, yet again.

This time with six pusher plates following us like a dog on a leash. I was mentally working through scenarios that would allow us to do all the setup and control from the hub side of the NGC-6934 portal. Nothing really seemed acceptable.

It looked like we were going into the lion's den again, like it or not. I noticed that Goku was uncharacteristically silent. It occurred to me that he might be going through the same mental gymnastics, and probably with the same results.

A day later we were again facing the portal to NGC 6934. I'd kind of hoped that familiarity might have started to breed contempt, but no. I still felt my knees go weak at the idea of transiting through to the cluster.

But it had to be done. We girded our loins, whatever that means, and dove through the portal. Nothing had really changed.

Still tons of stars in the sky. Still that peculiar distortion where the black hole should be. Still a magnificent view of the Milky Way from high

above the galactic plane. Okay, that last one was almost worth the price of entry. Setting up the pusher plates was no big deal.

Bill had provided documentation and the command set wasn't really extensive. In an hour, we had the wormhole and its satellites bracketed in an octahedral shape with the vertices directly over the satellites. Okay, Goku, let's move this puppy to the other side of the stellar system.

That should give Bill enough breathing room to get his plate inventory up high enough to test. Works for me. And executing.

Goku pressed the activate button. The pusher plate status window showed all six plates powering up and connecting the group's surge field around the wormhole and its satellites.

Then, Goku's eyes registered shock and he fairly screamed, what the fuck? I turned to his status window and felt my face

freeze. The wormhole had disappeared.
