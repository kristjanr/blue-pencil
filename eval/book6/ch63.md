It's a little odd that essentially immortal beings can still be subject to impatience, but we were human once, and it didn't seem like we would ever leave human behaviors behind.

Day and I, having come up with the plan, wanted to implement it as quickly as possible, so we'd unshipped our printers, sacrificed half our Buster inventory for raw materials, and built a warp drone. Bill had been enthusiastic, if a bit distracted. The war was taking some unexpected turns, apparently, but with Bill's sporadic help and the blueprints from his experimental rigs, we'd soon come up with the Mark I wormhole cedar.

A quick test, a few minutes out and a few minutes back, confirmed that everything worked. Now, we were at the Antimatter Fountain, seven light years from Sagittarius A-Star, the SMBH at the center of our galaxy.

We had the warp drone, we had a wormhole generator, and we had a couple of portal satellites ready to force-grow the wormhole at the other end.

And we had a problem. Maybe.

I'm not thrilled about putting our portal close to the Antimatter Fountain, I said. If for no other reason than the Central Antimatter Works Administrator might get uppity about it. What, it might call security?

Well, yes. Maybe. Okay, then, let's go.

Oh, yeah, we can't. I nodded, knowing where Day had been about to go. We couldn't fly a wormhole endpoint through the wormhole, so our portal was going to have to stay in this system, and if we started from another system, the trip to A-Star would take way longer, even at warp.

Well, okay, let's fly a light hour that-a-way and set up. The PGF doesn't own all of space, after all.

Fair enough, then we can launch the Wormhole Cedar first. Dae paused. Then we sit around for two months.

Want to play some D&D? I chuckled.

We could, but we will also need to build some warp spy drones. We have a lot of ground to cover, Dae. Anything interesting will probably be well outside the accretion disk.

I scowled, thinking it through. The visible part of the disk is about 20 light days radius, but there's still a lot of crap out to a much greater distance. We'd spent years scanning that on SURGE drive by ourselves.

Huh. You have been thinking about this, Dae nodded. We have a plan.

Let's do this. The actual operation took very little time. Generating a wormhole pair was probabilistic and could, in theory, take forever, but we hit the middle of the curve and generated one in a few hours.

One end went into a magnetic bottle in my hold and the other went into the drone, which we immediately launched toward Sagittarius A-star Then, because we were paranoid, we spent a couple of days flying straight away from the antimatter works There was simply no reason to expect the automated systems would even notice a portal this far away, let alone come out to shut it down

And with that, we were ready We frame-jacked way down to make the time pass faster and settled in for a D&D session
