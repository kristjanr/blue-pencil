The Milky Way galaxy floated in the middle of the room at one gabillionth scale, slowly rotating, 20 or so bobs sitting in various incarnations of lazy boys, Adirondacks, deck chairs, and beanbags, stared and raptured. Red dots in the 3D image indicated known wormhole locations and blue dots indicated hubs. I couldn't help smiling to myself just a little.

It was a pretty good mapping of my travels with day through the Pan-Galactic Federation's wormhole network. What set it apart from every other image of the Milky Way that I'd ever seen was the big red circle near the edge of the galaxy, directly opposite Earth's location. That was where Nemesis, a 200 million solar mass black hole, was coming in like an out-of-control semi aimed directly for the core.

It was a not-very-subtle reminder that this wasn't just about exploration for its own sake. While we were scouting around the PGF's network, we would also be keeping an eye open for anything that might help avert the existential danger Nemesis presented, and most specifically, any route through the network that would take us the closest to Nemesis.

It wasn't clear yet what the Bobs could actually do about the approaching black hole, but step one was being able to get there, and that network still had a lot of gaps, which several people in the group had already taken unreasonable delight in pointing out.
