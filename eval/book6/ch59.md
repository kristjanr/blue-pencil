All the unknowns seemed to have reached a boiling point in my brain. There were too many unanswered questions. About the PGF, about the warp drive, about the wormhole network, about the exodus, about the great upheaval.

Some of these questions were relevant to our future, many more were critical to our immediate present, and I decided it was time for me to get some answers. Or, at least, try to. The war was down to a slow simmer.

Medeiros and the defenders would trade blows and battlefronts would shift this way or that, but for the moment things had shifted into something like a stalemate. Medeiros might still prevail in the end if he had some tricks of his own still in reserve. What that meant for the future of humanity was unknown.

For the Bobiverse and for replicants in general, the picture would be significantly bleaker. It wasn't quite at the level of a Spockian act of desperation, but it was certainly time for me to have my long, delayed conversation with the archivist. It had been literally centuries since I'd been out of the Epsilon Eridani system.

Physically, anyway. By now I'd been everywhere by mani one way or the other, but for this jaunt I'd had to transfer my Matrix to a Titan hull. There was something viscerally different about committing your Matrix to the wild.

It took a couple of days before I found myself in the Abydos system, the PGF capital. By now our ships were a known quantity and tolerated, if not welcomed. No marching bands, but no getting shot at either.

Icarus and Daedalus had asked the archivist a lot of questions but their focus was on exploration. Mine was more on research and history so I expected to be able to get at least some new information. Per Icky's instructions I had set up a sandboxed Bob avatar to handle the actual communication so I wouldn't go postal with the delays.

Icky said to treat it like a conversation by text. I reviewed my list of questions then activated the avatar. I am Bill Johansson of the United Federation of Sentients.

I am a digital. Will you answer questions for me? Yes, subject to the usual limitations for non-members.

Well, that was a good start. Do you maintain state between each question for a supplicant, or is each question a new conversation? My avatar asked.

State is maintained, the archivist replied. Subject to a timeout of 100 standard hours.

Do all member species have equal access to technology? That information is not available. That didn't take long.

Agreed, the archivist said. I chuckled. Definitely no sense of irony there, but I was going to have to break through this reflexive blockading.

Would you have answered this question for the human representative, Senator Newberg? Yes. Do you agree that I belong to the same civilization?

As Senator Newberg, keeping in mind that those like me are the reason you rejected her application? Yes. So in principle, I could bring her back and feed her the questions and she would ask them and you would answer them.

Agreed. So what purpose does it serve to deny me the answers? Is delay a deliberate strategy?

There was a pause. Based on what Icarus had reported, that was a good thing. The point is valid.

I will answer questions for you that I would have answered for Senator Newberg. Yes! I jumped around and did some random fist pumps.

To reiterate, my avatar continued, do all member species have equal access to available Federation technology? Yes. Do all member species make use of FTL drive?

FTL is not an available technology. The Pan-Galactic Federation doesn't have FTL? That is correct.

I stared at the transcript on the video window totally boggled. That couldn't be right. There had to be some misunderstanding.

I quickly modified the avatar's script. How are new wormhole paths laid out? Member species connect to the wormhole network as they make contact with and join the federation.

What about the hubs? The network consists primarily of rings and spokes. Very few new hubs have been added since before the creation of the federation.

Why not? That information is not available. I glared at the response, then had second thoughts.

That had been a poorly phrased question, certainly too general for a non-sentient AI to deal with. I tried again. Is there a technical or political reason for the slow pace of expansion of the wormhole network?

As of the date of the exodus, the Federation had not expanded into the entire existing wormhole network. There was no motivation for further expansion. The Federation didn't create the network?

No. Then who did? That information is not available.

Why would this information in particular be classified? It is not classified. That information is simply not available.

Oh. Oh, hell. The PGF had inherited the wormhole network.

Wait, that couldn't be right either. The shepherding satellites are PGF tech, though, aren't they? my avatar asked

The units in place when the wormholes were discovered were replaced with PGF satellites as the security on the original units could not be bypassed Well, sure, I guess if you didn't have the passwords you were SOL

but once the original satellites had been scanned the engineering principles would have been straightforward to reproduce Then I had an idea I pulled up some of Calvin's images from the Dutchman showing the text on the satellites

Please examine the attached image Do you recognize the text? Yes, that script is similar to what was inscribed on the original satellites based on archival footage.

Some artifacts have also been found with similar symbols. No further information is available. No translation is available.

Holy hell. The network had been created by an elder race. Progenitors.

Heechi, the ancient ones. The PGF were essentially squatters. I wasn't sure how it really affected anything except that the Dutchman obviously wasn't a PGF vessel.

which explained why the PGF left as early as they did. They still wouldn't have arrived at the large Magellanic Cloud when Nemesis plowed into the Milky Way. On the other hand, they wouldn't need to build ships that would last 170,000 years, thanks to Einstein.

But we now had to reckon with a progenitor race. Whereabouts unknown? The ex-PGF digitals.

Whereabouts unknown? And the entire PGF population, hightailing it for the greater Magellanic Cloud because of an incoming galaxy-destroying black hole. Turned out that however exciting we thought the times we were living in, we badly underestimated it.

Garfield was going to love this.
