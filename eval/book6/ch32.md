I had close to a dozen different designs for Alcubierre drives, all based on different equations, using different assumptions about how space would react to the insult.

These had been generally worked out as early as the late 20th century, at least in theoretical terms. I could eliminate about half of the designs right away, just based on conflicts with the design of the derelict, which I had started calling the Dutchman. That didn't necessarily mean that those variations were unworkable, just that the Dutchman clearly didn't use any of them.

and without some clue as to potential designs, I couldn't test them anyway. But that still left me a lot of options. I immediately implemented construction on all the remaining designs with my auto factories and roamers working in parallel.

It might take a little longer overall before the first contender would be ready, but it would be considerably quicker to try out all the alternatives, unless I lucked out on the first one, and that was a chance I was willing to take. A chance I wasn't willing to take, though, was activating any of the test platforms anywhere near Ragnarok or Ultima Thule. Way out in the Oort cloud seemed good enough, unless the experiment started unraveling space or collapsing the false vacuum or something.

In that case, it really wouldn't matter where we were, so screw it.

The first attempt was a dismal failure.

Not a spectacular one exactly. The entire test rig simply disappeared.

None of the sensors surrounding the test field indicated anything. There was no energy discharge, massive or otherwise, just poof, gone.

I'd set up the test firing to last for only 10 microseconds, so on the assumption that it had gone somewhere I waited 24 hours for the beacon transmission to reach me. Nothing.

It was gone for good, apparently. Where? Who knew?

Another dimension? Time travel to the distant past? It didn't matter really.

There were easier ways to get rid of equipment. The second attempt was more spectacular, if not any more useful. On activation, the test vessel spread itself over a thousand kilometers or so along the flight line.

What was left was more or less a vapor trail of iron, nickel, platinum, and other elements in a long, thin cloud Say, this is not going well, Garfield commented after the second test You think?

I paused, rolling my eyes But this isn't unexpected, it's always been assumed that most configurations of an Alcubierre drive would be less than useful Yeah, two down, six to go

Better hope you have one of the useful ones in your list Well, that is a concern, Gar There's no reason to believe we've thought of every possible configuration, we could have completely missed the boat

Or there could be no configuration that works. Uh, no. The Dutchman obviously worked.

So there is, in principle, at least one variation on an Alcubierre drive that does work, we just have to find it. Now it was Garfield's turn to roll his eyes. We next tried version 3, and finally got the massive explosion we'd been anticipating and fearing.

Fortunately, I had set up our test area more than 10,000 AU out. Any explosion large enough to affect Ragnarok from that distance probably would have taken out the spiral arm. And anyway, there just wasn't enough energy in the system to produce that kind of boom.

Although this one was probably energetic enough to send Oort objects into the inner system in a few hundred thousand years.

I mentioned this to Garfield and he replied, Honestly, Bill, there isn't enough power in the test rig to produce the explosion we actually got. So there's energy feeding in from somewhere else.

I find that slightly concerning.

You and me both, Gar. I think at some point we're going to have to get the ex-human physics community involved. Why haven't we already?

I hesitated, staring at a spot on the wall for a moment. Um, I think I may have picked up a little of that paranoia from Howard and Will. I know we've always felt that information should be free, but between the SUDDAR cloaking, wormholes, Skywalkers, and now this, it just feels like there's a time and place, and we aren't there yet.

Wow, Garfield shook his head but didn't pursue the point. Plus, I added, and maybe more to the point, I don't want to have egg on my face if none of this works out.

Okay, that I can get behind, I gave Garfield a tired smile. One more thing. Oh, God, what?

A working FTL drive would potentially make dealing with it so much easier. We don't have time for leisurely research and figuring it all out ourselves, so maybe the archivist can give us a leg up. I paused and gave him a sickly grin.

As to the when, that'd be as soon as I have two mils to rub together.
