Our application has been rejected. Senator Newberg's image in the comms window was emotionless. I had to believe she was doing that for effect.

Good poker face, honestly. Did they give you a reason? She looked down and now managed to appear embarrassed.

The reason stated was the high level of digitals in our society. Wait, replicants? Mostly.

They weren't happy about ANEC and THOTH, but recognized that those were exceptional situations. Newberg paused. And THOTH isn't really part of our society anyway.

I don't understand. I frowned at the video. They have a problem with digital intelligences of all kinds?

What exactly is the archivist? The archivist isn't sentient by its own admission. It started to discuss specifics, but the whole thing quickly got beyond me.

I've received a pricey, which I'll give to a cybernetics expert when we get back. But the bottom line is that the AIs in the PGF are something like AMIs with common sense or replicants without free will. They have a job.

They're built to do that job. They want to do that job. They're very good at that job.

And they have no desire to do anything else. They are referred to as Manufactured Dedicated Intelligences, MDIs. That's the English translation, of course.

Okay, what's the issue with digitals? That's the word they use for AIs and replicants. You—Newberg paused and looked embarrassed.

Look, I'm sorry to make this personal, but you are a replicant, so this is about you. And all the Bobiverse, and all the post-life arcologies, for that matter, essentially, to the PGF? You're all forbidden tech.

I was flabbergasted. I actually stared at Senator Newberg for several whole seconds before I replied, What the fuck?

She sighed and screwed up her face for a moment before meeting my eyes. Howard, something happened about 6,000 years ago. The archivist won't give details because as non-members we're not privy to that level of information, but because I'm a bio and because it's relevant to the rejection of our application, the archivist was able to give me a little more info.

She took a deep breath before continuing.

The PGF followed a technological path very similar to humanity, which included replication and AI.

Unlike humanity and the Quinlans, though, they decided not to pursue the AGI path, so they never developed something like Thoth or Anik. Or possibly they did and it blew up in their faces. The archivist gets evasive around the subject, but they did pursue replication.

I mean, why not? It's a form of immortality. But around 6,000 years ago, things came to a head in some way.

Not outright war, the archivist admitted things didn't get to violence, but there was what I'd have to call an acrimonious divorce Again, Newberg paused The end result is that replication was outlawed

By the way, it also answers the question of whether the citizens of the PGF are active while in transit They aren't To do so would make them replicants

Instead, they are paused That's the word the archivist used And they'll just be reconstituted at the other end?

I said, disbelief in my voice Yes, along with their planets Say what?

Newberg chuckled The PGF is far ahead of us technologically, at least in that area. They can reconstruct their planets once they reach their destinations and then reconstitute the population and life will go on.

But the populations were all gone, everywhere. Presumably that means the scan was destructive. Why are the planets still there?

That's hopefully dealt with in the pricey I was given, but the archivists did say that they needed to be sure that they transported the originals rather than just making copies on arrival, whereas with the planets, it didn't matter. Something Bill had mentioned at one point tugged at the edge of my memory. I made a note to discuss this with him.

And soon.

Fine. So, is there any way to get approval for membership? Yes.

Get rid of all the replicants.

Oh, wonderful. I stopped what I was about to say, then said instead.

On the other hand, a lot of humans would be happy to do that. How gone would we have to be? Newberg looked surprised, probably at the thought that I might be considering this alternative.

Uh, it seems to be about influencing bio-society. The fact that replicants can vote and own property and pay taxes seems to be a major factor. Son of a bitch, I muttered.

Starfleet was right. Excuse me? Sorry, Senator, not relevant.

So what's the bottom line? If we move all the post-life arcologies and the Bobiverse-Severs contact, can you reapply? You're seriously considering this?

Not really, no. Even in just my case, my wife and I have raised any number of adopted children who are part of human society. We're not about to walk away from them.

Plus, we have businesses, friends. We're members of various societies. I know ex-humans who are still teaching, who are heavily involved in human society.

Bob's friend Teresa is trying to set up diplomatic relations with the PAV. I shook my head slowly. I don't see you telling all these people they have to go away and that ending well.

Not that it matters, Howard. The archivist quite reasonably pointed out that it would have no way to verify our claims if we came back anyway. It told me flat out that a reapplication would not be considered until the PGF government was reintegrated and there would have to be in-person inspections

Until they get to the Large Magellanic Cloud and set themselves up, you mean? I'm afraid so Then, what now?

Well, the archivist admits that they don't have any kind of standing navy MDIs aren't allowed to run a military or use any force beyond policing activities, so they won't attack us or chase us out of the PGF But they also won't give us access to the communications system, for instance, or answer endless questions

So we're cut off? More or less. The archivist will still talk to us and can still answer some questions, but it says the scope of allowable subjects has mostly been mined already.

Meaning? Meaning it's answered most of the questions it's prepared to answer. I sat back and stared at the ceiling.

Fuck. The flight back to Vulcan passed with minimal communications. I'm sure Senator Newberg was putting together a report for the UFS Council, and I was honestly embarrassed.

I couldn't really figure out why I should be, logically or even morally, but it was a weird feeling to realize that both Starfleet and the general run of xenophobic humanity would be crowing in triumph when this got out, claiming we were nothing but trouble and they told you so. I could even hear the capital letters in that last bit. As soon as we re-entered the Vulcan system through the portal, I beamed a formal goodbye to the Dauntless and sent a message to Bill.

This was going to need a meeting, and it was going to be a doozy.
