That's a starfield, right? Goku said. The transmitted image, grainy as it was, showed what looked like stars

in part of the video frame. Yeah, and it doesn't look like a view through a window either, too ragged. It was immediately obvious that the other end of the wormhole was inside something, very probably a ship's

Cargo Bay. It was also obvious, despite the darkness, that the opening through which we could see the stars wasn't supposed to be there. It had a very definite hole-ripped-through-the-hull feeling to it, which really raised more questions than it answered.

I played with the controls. I'm going to expand both sides up to baseball size. I want more resolution.

Then we'll have the roamer take a full spherical 360 scan. Or, Goku said, uh-huh, have the T-1000 extend the camera through and do that scan. Huh, I nodded.

And as a bonus, we get to play with it. The T-1000, whose experimental name was Skywalker for some reason, took some getting used to. Controlling it was more like creating a 3D animation than simply giving orders, but finally we had it doing what we wanted.

The Skywalker stuck a pseudopod through the wormhole with a camera and the flashlight on the end, then proceeded to do orange slice rotations. We let it finish, then projected the result around us, Stellarium style. Nope, I said, that's just not right.

Half the cargo bay wall's been ripped off, Goku replied. No redshift in the starfield, I added. And, I waited for several seconds.

I'm seeing rotation. It's a derelict. We need to get a bird's eye view, I nodded.

We can push a roamer through. If it curls up first, I'm sending a couple more to our portal. We'll set one up as a relay at the mouth.

We were careful to instruct the roamers to return in case of loss of communications, but we needn't have worried. Two roamers on either side of the wormhole converted SCUT to radio, then back. Bandwidth could, in principle, have become a problem, but we weren't doing more than nudging the remote roamers occasionally.

The roamers took almost an hour to do a complete survey, inside and out. Again, we projected the results in surround vision.

That's a weird shape for a ship, Goku opined. Yes, but we can't make any assumptions. We can make engineering assumptions, Cal.

Ship designs are constrained by drive system, weapons, cargo, and so on. This one, well, it certainly has cargo, Goku snorted. The hold, we'd discovered, contained row after row of linked-up satellites, likely with microscopic wormholes suspended electromagnetically in their centers.

The wormhole assemblies were arranged in pairs, and a label on each one of the paired items differed only in one character, and the same two characters, like everything had been labeled something A and something B. The rest of it was probably a numeric designator, and probably, because we couldn't read the alien script. It didn't correspond to anything that Ick and Day had picked up on their first exploration of the PGF.

That didn't mean it wasn't a PGF member species, but it did raise some questions.

We also did an astronomical survey and determined that the ship was about 40 light hours away, going straight out from the core, and not traveling all that fast, strangely.

Or maybe not so strangely, given the degree of damage to the ship. Something had essentially torn a strip off one side, taking off perhaps a third of the ship's mass. It appeared to be an impact of some kind, and a major one.

It had probably completely overwhelmed the ship's self-repair capability. I commented distractedly. The surge field is supposed to protect a ship from random matter.

How did whatever it was get through the field? Good question. Could they have turned off the surge drive?

Just a case of bad timing? What? Why?

They're not even going that fast. They'd—I stopped, then my eyes narrowed. Based on velocity and distance from the system we're in, this ship has been drifting for about 23,000 years.

That's longer than the PGF has been around. That tracks, Goku replied, and it might explain the unreadable script, but it gives us a new load of questions to hand to Ick and Dae. Huh.

A whole lot of people are going to have to get involved. So what now? I pondered for a second.

We don't know how to control the PGF satellites, if that's who they belong to, but as it happens, we have a whole crap ton of our own. Let's just disconnect a couple from the generator and push them through, then get the roamers to transfer the wormhole kernel into our satellites. Then we can expand it with minimal effort.

No sooner said than done. We pushed a half dozen more roamers through the small wormhole, then had them unship the remote kernel and move it out of the derelict. Once done, we were able to force the remote wormhole open far enough to push through a couple of our portal satellites.

After that, it was all basically routine.

Two days later, we had a wormhole with a half kilometer diameter, connecting us to the patch of nowhere containing the alien ship. We probably should have played it safe and had one of us stay on this side of our wormhole, but neither of us was willing to volunteer. And anyway, in the worst-case scenario where the wormhole collapsed, we could fly back to this location in about a week, given the Titan-class horsepower.

We circled the Hulk a few times, but there really wasn't anything new to learn. What now? I said.

Bring it home? Uh, I thought for a moment. No tractor beams and no tie-downs on our hulls, according to the blueprints.

Drones can tow? Goku replied. Bob's spun up farming donuts using drone teams back in the day.

Blogs again? Yep. Still not too late to learn to read.

Ignoring the jibe, I checked inventory. I have twelve drones in stock. Presumably you have the same.

Even with that, acceleration will be snail-like. But I guess we can leave them to it with proper instructions. I think I'd like to be there to control jumps, though.

Fair enough. Let's set this up. Getting things set up didn't take long since we weren't especially concerned about where we put attachment points on the alien Hulk.

Getting back to human space was going to be more of a problem. For one thing, the Hulk was traveling away from the galaxy and that vector would persist after transiting the wormhole. The drones would have to bleed off that velocity before they could aim for the next portal.

This was going to be a slow process.

We did some stress tests on the towing systems, then activated the program for the drones. All the cables went taut as they started pulling. Now what?

I said. Well, this first jump is no biggie since our wormhole is right there, but bleeding off that vector in 6-3-0 to hit the next portal will take a couple weeks minimum, Goku thought for a moment. Let's get the first jump done.

Set up the instructions for the second jump, then do some exploring. Maybe whip over to spoke one and follow it outbound? You're not worried about leaving the Hulk on its own?

What are you concerned about, space pirates? Come on, Cal, the PGF is deserted. More likely one of the other bobs will stumble upon it.

We'll leave a buoy attached to tell everyone to back off. How's that? I sighed.

Yeah, good enough. And yes, I'm being paranoid. I mean, we've both watched the same movies, right?

Goku chuckled. I'd almost be disappointed if nothing happens. A final set of instructions to our mechanical

servants, and we were off.
